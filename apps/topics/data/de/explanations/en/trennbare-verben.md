Separable and inseparable verbs are a fundamental feature of German that changes how the verb appears in a sentence depending on whether you use the infinitive, a conjugated form, or a past participle. Understanding which prefix stays attached and which separates from the verb stem is essential for correct German word order and meaning.

## What Are Separable and Inseparable Verbs

Separable verbs have a prefix that detaches from the verb stem when the verb is conjugated in main clauses. The prefix moves to the end of the clause, while the conjugated verb stays in its normal position. Inseparable verbs have a prefix that never separates from the verb stem, no matter how the sentence is structured. The prefix remains permanently attached.

## Separable Verbs - Formation and Word Order

- In the infinitive, the prefix and verb are joined: anrufen, aussteigen, einkaufen, fernsehen, mitnehmen.
- When conjugated in a main clause, the prefix jumps to the end: Ich rufe dich an. (I call you.) Sie steigen hier aus. (They get out here.)
- In questions, the conjugated verb comes first and the prefix still goes to the end: Rufst du mich an? (Are you calling me?) Steigen Sie hier aus? (Are you getting out here?)
- In negative sentences, the prefix moves to the end before or after the negation: Ich rufe dich nicht an. (I am not calling you.)
- In subordinate clauses with a conjugated verb, the prefix reunites with the verb at the end: Ich weiß, dass du mich anrufst. (I know that you are calling me.)

## Separable Verbs - With Compound Tenses and Infinitive Constructions

- In the present perfect and past perfect, the past participle keeps the prefix attached and adds ge in the middle: angerufen, ausgestiegen, eingekauft. Er hat mich angerufen. (He called me.) Sie waren ausgestiegen. (They had gotten out.)
- With modal verbs, the infinitive is not separated in the infinitive phrase: Ich kann dich anrufen. (I can call you.) But remember the infinitive stays whole even though it is at the end.
- Common separable prefixes include: ab, an, auf, aus, bei, ein, fest, her, hin, los, mit, nach, vor, weg, zu, zurück, zusammen.

## Inseparable Verbs - Definition and Recognition

- Inseparable verbs have prefixes that never detach from the verb stem, even in conjugated or past participle forms.
- The infinitive, conjugated form, past participle, and all other forms keep the prefix attached: verstehen, verstehe, verstanden (understand), beschreiben, beschreibe, beschrieben (describe).
- In main clauses, the conjugated verb with its prefix takes the normal position: Ich verstehe dich. (I understand you.) Sie beschreiben das Problem. (They describe the problem.)
- In questions, the entire verb (prefix plus stem) inverts: Verstehst du das? (Do you understand that?)
- In subordinate clauses, the whole verb stays intact at the end: Ich weiß, dass du das verstehst. (I know that you understand that.)

## Common Inseparable Prefixes and Their Meanings

- be: verstehen, bearbeiten, besuchen (understand, work on, visit).
- ent: entdecken, entfernen, entlassen (discover, remove, dismiss).
- er: erkennen, erhalten, erklären (recognize, receive, explain).
- ge: gefallen, gedeihen, gelingen (like, thrive, succeed).
- miss: missverständnis, misstrauen (misunderstanding, distrust).
- über: überwinden, überleben (overcome, survive).
- um: umgeben, umschreiben (surround, paraphrase).
- un: unternehmen, unterbrechen (undertake, interrupt).
- ver: vergessen, verlassen, versuchen (forget, leave, try).
- zer: zerfallen, zerstören (fall apart, destroy).

## Verbs That Can Be Both Separable and Inseparable

- Some verbs with certain prefixes change meaning based on whether the prefix is separable or inseparable. The prefix über in übersetzen means to ferry across (separable) versus to translate (inseparable). Ich setze dich über. (I ferry you across.) Ich übersetze das Buch. (I translate the book.)
- Similarly, umgeben means to go around (separable) or to surround (inseparable). Wir geben das Haus um. (We go around the house.) Berge umgeben das Tal. (Mountains surround the valley.)
- The verb durchbrechen has both meanings: Ich breche durch die Mauer durch. (I break through the wall—separable, action in space.) Die Sonne durchbricht die Wolken. (The sun breaks through the clouds—inseparable, figurative.)

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Separating inseparable verbs: Ich ver-gesse das. ✗ (Correct: Ich vergesse das.)
- Not separating separable verbs in main clauses: Ich anrufe dich. ✗ (Correct: Ich rufe dich an.)
- Separating the prefix in subordinate clauses: Ich weiß, dass du mich an-rufst. ✗ (Correct: Ich weiß, dass du mich anrufst.)
- Forgetting the ge in the past participle of separable verbs: Ich habe dich angeruft. ✓ versus Ich habe dich anruf. ✗ or Ich habe dich gerufen. ✗
- Confusing the prefix position in infinitive phrases with modal verbs: Ich kann dich an-rufen. ✗ (Correct: Ich kann dich anrufen.)
- Misplacing the prefix in questions: Rufst du an mich? ✗ (Correct: Rufst du mich an?)
