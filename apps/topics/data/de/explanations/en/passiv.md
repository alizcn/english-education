The passive voice in German shifts focus from the person or thing performing an action to the action itself or the person or thing receiving it. Instead of saying who does something, you describe what happens. German uses the auxiliary verb werden with the past participle to form the passive, and this construction is more common in German writing and formal speech than in English.

## When the Passive is Used

The passive voice appears frequently in German when the agent (the person doing the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from context. In newspapers, academic writing, and formal instructions, passives dominate because they create an objective tone. You also use the passive when you want to emphasize the action or the receiver rather than the doer.

- Das Fenster wurde geöffnet. (The window was opened.)
- Die Hausaufgaben müssen bis Freitag gemacht werden. (The homework must be done by Friday.)
- Hier wird Deutsch gesprochen. (German is spoken here.)

## Building the Passive: The Positive Form

The German passive consists of two parts: the auxiliary verb werden in the appropriate tense, plus the past participle of the main verb. The past participle always comes at the end of the sentence or clause.

- Präsens: Das Auto wird repariert. (The car is being repaired.)
- Präteritum: Das Auto wurde repariert. (The car was repaired.)
- Perfekt: Das Auto ist repariert worden. (The car has been repaired.)
- Plusquamperfekt: Das Auto war repariert worden. (The car had been repaired.)

Note that in the perfect and pluperfect tenses, the past participle of werden changes to worden instead of geworden. This is one of the key spelling rules learners must remember.

- Das Buch wurde von meinem Freund geschrieben. (The book was written by my friend.)
- Die Pizza wird um 7 Uhr geliefert. (The pizza will be delivered at 7 o'clock.)

## Negation in the Passive

To negate a passive sentence, place nicht before the past participle or use kein/keine with a noun. The structure mirrors active negation, but the position of nicht matters because the past participle is at the end.

- Das Auto wurde nicht repariert. (The car was not repaired.)
- Das Auto wird nicht repariert. (The car is not being repaired.)
- Kein Brief wurde geschrieben. (No letter was written.)

## Questions in the Passive

To form a question in the passive, invert the finite form of werden with the subject. The past participle stays at the end of the sentence.

- Wird das Auto repariert? (Is the car being repaired?)
- Wurde das Haus gebaut? (Was the house built?)
- Wird die Prüfung morgen geschrieben? (Will the exam be written tomorrow?)

## Tenses and Time Expressions

German learners sometimes struggle with which form of werden to use. The tense of werden determines when the action happens, not the form of the past participle. Present passive uses wird/werde/werdet/werden, past passive uses wurde/wurdest/wurdet/wurden, and perfect passive uses ist/bin/bist/seid + past participle + worden.

- Morgen wird das Projekt präsentiert. (Tomorrow the project will be presented.)
- Letzten Monat wurde die Straße renoviert. (Last month the street was renovated.)
- Die Häuser sind in den 1980er Jahren gebaut worden. (The houses were built in the 1980s.)

## Agent and the Word von

When you want to mention who performed the action, use von followed by the dative case. This is optional, but when it appears, it always follows von. In English we use "by," but von requires the dative, not the accusative.

- Das Lied wurde von Goethe geschrieben. (The song was written by Goethe.)
- Der Kuchen wurde von meiner Mutter gebacken. (The cake was baked by my mother.)
- Das Projekt wird von unserer Gruppe gelöst. (The project is being solved by our group.)

## Common Learner Mistakes

A frequent error is forgetting that worden, not geworden, is used in perfect and pluperfect passives. English speakers sometimes carry over their language's passive structure by forgetting to change word order or by using sein instead of werden as the auxiliary. Another mistake is placing the past participle in the wrong position or misidentifying which verb form to use in the present tense. Learners also confuse the passive voice with the stative passive (sein + participle), which expresses a state or condition rather than an action.

- Falsch: Das Auto ist repariert geworden. (Wrong ending)
- Richtig: Das Auto ist repariert worden.
- Falsch: Der Brief schreibt von mir. (Active word order and wrong verb)
- Richtig: Der Brief wird von mir geschrieben.
- Falsch: Wird gearbitet? (Wrong participle form)
- Richtig: Wird gearbeitet?
