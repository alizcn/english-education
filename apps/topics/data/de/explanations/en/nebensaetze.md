Subordinate clauses introduced by weil, dass, and wenn are essential structures in German that allow you to express reasons, statements, conditions, and temporal relationships. All three use the same fundamental principle: the finite verb must move to the end of the clause, which is the defining characteristic of German subordinate clauses.

## The Weil Clause: Expressing Reasons

Use weil to give a reason or explanation for the main clause.

- Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil das Wetter schlecht ist. (I stay home because the weather is bad.)
- Sie kommt nicht, weil sie krank ist. (She doesn't come because she is sick.)
- Wir fahren nach Berlin, weil die Stadt sehr schön ist. (We drive to Berlin because the city is very beautiful.)

The weil clause can appear at the beginning or end of the sentence. When it begins the sentence, the main clause word order inverts slightly:

- Weil das Wetter schlecht ist, bleibe ich zu Hause. (Because the weather is bad, I stay home.)
- Weil sie krank ist, kommt sie nicht. (Because she is sick, she doesn't come.)

For negation, add nicht before the element you negate:

- Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil das Wetter nicht schön ist. (I stay home because the weather is not nice.)

## The Dass Clause: Reporting Thoughts and Facts

Use dass to introduce reported speech, beliefs, facts, or ideas. This clause often follows verbs of thinking, saying, knowing, or believing.

- Ich denke, dass er recht hat. (I think that he is right.)
- Sie sagt, dass die Arbeit schwierig ist. (She says that the work is difficult.)
- Ich weiß, dass du morgen kommst. (I know that you are coming tomorrow.)
- Es ist wichtig, dass wir pünktlich sind. (It is important that we are punctual.)

The dass clause frequently appears after verbs like denken, sagen, wissen, glauben, meinen, hoffen, and with impersonal expressions like es ist wichtig, es ist möglich.

For negation, place nicht before the verb or the element negated:

- Ich denke, dass er nicht recht hat. (I think that he is not right.)
- Sie sagt, dass die Arbeit nicht schwierig ist. (She says that the work is not difficult.)

Questions within dass clauses maintain the subordinate word order with the verb at the end:

- Sie fragen, ob die Schule offen ist. (They ask whether the school is open.)
- Ich möchte wissen, was du machst. (I would like to know what you are doing.)

## The Wenn Clause: Conditions and Temporal Events

Wenn serves two functions: it introduces conditional statements (if) and temporal statements (when/whenever).

For conditions:

- Wenn es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause. (If it rains, I stay home.)
- Wenn du Zeit hast, komm mich besuchen. (If you have time, come visit me.)
- Ich gehe ins Kino, wenn ich Geld habe. (I go to the cinema if I have money.)

For temporal events (when something happens regularly or at a specific time):

- Wenn die Sonne aufgeht, wache ich auf. (When the sun rises, I wake up.)
- Wenn er anruft, bin ich immer zu Hause. (When he calls, I am always home.)

Negation with wenn works like the other subordinate clauses:

- Wenn es nicht regnet, gehe ich spazieren. (If it does not rain, I go for a walk.)
- Wenn du keine Zeit hast, verstehe ich das. (If you don't have time, I understand that.)

For counterfactual conditions (contrary to reality), use the subjunctive mood with wenn:

- Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich dich besuchen. (If I had time, I would visit you.)
- Wenn das möglich wäre, würde ich es machen. (If that were possible, I would do it.)

## Word Order in Subordinate Clauses

The critical rule for all subordinate clauses is that the finite verb moves to the very end. This is different from main clauses, where the finite verb is in the second position.

Main clause: Ich gehe heute ins Kino. (I go to the cinema today.)

Subordinate clause: ...weil ich heute ins Kino gehe. (...because I today go to the cinema.)

With separable verbs, the entire verb phrase goes to the end:

- Ich rufe dich an. (I call you up.)
- ...weil ich dich anrufe. (...because I you up-call.)

With modal verbs and the perfect tense, both parts go to the end:

- Ich muss arbeiten. (I must work.)
- ...weil ich arbeiten muss. (...because I work must.)
- Ich bin gegangen. (I have gone.)
- ...weil ich gegangen bin. (...because I gone am.)

## Common Learner Mistakes

The most frequent error is placing the finite verb in the wrong position. Learners often keep the verb in second position as in main clauses:

- WRONG: ...weil ich gehe ins Kino.
- RIGHT: ...weil ich ins Kino gehe.

Another common mistake is forgetting that separable prefixes rejoin the verb at the end:

- WRONG: Wenn ich dich an-rufe später...
- RIGHT: Wenn ich dich später anrufe...

Learners sometimes confuse weil and denn. Remember: weil introduces a subordinate clause with verb-final word order, while denn is a coordinating conjunction that does not change word order:

- Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil es regnet. (subordinate)
- Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn es regnet. (coordinate, both clauses have normal word order)

Another mistake involves forgetting the comma before the subordinate clause, which is always required in German:

- WRONG: Ich gehe spazieren wenn das Wetter schön ist.
- RIGHT: Ich gehe spazieren, wenn das Wetter schön ist.

With dass clauses, learners sometimes omit dass when reporting speech, or incorrectly use an infinitive construction where dass is required:

- ACCEPTABLE: Sie sagt, dass die Arbeit schwierig ist.
- LESS FORMAL: Sie sagt, die Arbeit ist schwierig.
- WRONG: Sie sagt zu machen die Arbeit schwierig.

## Building Complex Sentences with Multiple Clauses

You can combine multiple subordinate clauses in one sentence. Each retains its own verb-final word order:

- Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich denke, dass es regnet. (I stay home because I think that it is raining.)
- Wenn du kommst und wenn ich Zeit habe, gehen wir ins Kino. (If you come and if I have time, we go to the cinema.)

When the main clause follows the subordinate clause, remember that the main clause's verb comes right after the subordinate clause's verb, and the subject of the main clause immediately follows:

- Weil es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause. (Because it rains, I stay home.)
- Wenn er anruft, antworte ich sofort. (When he calls, I answer immediately.)
