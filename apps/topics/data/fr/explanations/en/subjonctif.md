The French present subjunctive (subjonctif présent) is a mood used to express uncertainty, possibility, necessity, emotion, or doubt. Unlike the indicative, which states facts, the subjunctive conveys subjectivity and appears mainly in subordinate clauses introduced by specific verbs or expressions.

## When You Use the Subjunctive

The subjunctive appears after verbs and expressions that signal:

- Doubt or denial: Je doute qu'il vienne. (I doubt he comes.) / Je ne crois pas qu'elle soit là. (I don't think she is there.)
- Necessity or obligation: Il faut que tu partes. (You must leave.) / C'est nécessaire que nous commencions tôt. (It's necessary that we start early.)
- Emotion or desire: Je veux que tu réussisses. (I want you to succeed.) / Elle a peur qu'il pleuve. (She is afraid it will rain.)
- Judgment or opinion: C'est dommage que vous partiez. (It's a shame that you're leaving.) / Je préfère qu'elle appelle demain. (I prefer that she calls tomorrow.)

The subjunctive rarely appears in the main clause; it lives in "que" clauses after the main verb.

## How to Form the Regular Subjunctive

For most verbs, take the present indicative stem of the ils/elles form and add subjunctive endings.

Take parler: ils/elles parlent → remove -ent to get parl-. Then add:

- je parle
- tu parles
- il/elle/on parle
- nous parlions
- vous parliez
- ils/elles parlent

Notice that the first three singular forms and the third-person plural look identical to the present indicative. The nous and vous forms use the imparfait stem (parlions, parliez).

Another example with finir: ils/elles finissent → finiss-:

- je finisse
- tu finisses
- il/elle/on finisse
- nous finissions
- vous finissiez
- ils/elles finissent

## Common Irregular Verbs

Some verbs have irregular subjunctive stems and must be memorized:

- aller: Je veux que tu ailles au marché. (I want you to go to the market.)
- avoir: Il faut que tu aies ton passeport. (You must have your passport.)
- être: Je doute qu'il soit malade. (I doubt he is sick.)
- faire: Elle insiste pour que tu fasses tes devoirs. (She insists that you do your homework.)
- pouvoir: Je ne crois pas qu'il puisse venir. (I don't think he can come.)
- vouloir: Je préfère qu'elle veuille rester. (I prefer that she wants to stay.)
- savoir: Il faut que vous sachiez la vérité. (You must know the truth.)

Verbs like devoir, venir, tenir, and others also have irregular forms in the subjunctive.

## Negative and Interrogative Forms

To make the subjunctive negative, place ne...pas around the verb in the que clause:

- Je veux qu'il ne parte pas. (I want him not to leave.)
- Il faut que vous ne soyez pas en retard. (You must not be late.)

Questions with the subjunctive are rare in everyday speech, but they follow normal interrogative patterns:

- Crois-tu qu'il vienne? (Do you think he will come?) — This uses the subjunctive because of the doubt implied.
- Veux-tu que je te aide? (Do you want me to help you?) — Subject inversion in the main clause; the que clause is unchanged.

## Common Triggers and Subordinate Clauses

The subjunctive appears after these common expressions:

- Verbs of necessity: il faut que, il est nécessaire que, c'est important que
- Verbs of emotion: j'ai peur que, j'aime que, je déteste que, j'espère que (though espérer often takes indicative)
- Verbs of will: vouloir que, désirer que, préférer que, insister pour que
- Verbs of doubt: douter que, nier que, je ne crois pas que, je ne suis pas sûr que
- Impersonal constructions: c'est dommage que, c'est possible que, il se peut que, c'est regrettable que

Always use the subjunctive in the que clause that follows these triggers, even if the main subject and subordinate subject are different:

- Je veux que tu viennes. (Main subject: je; subordinate subject: tu)
- Ils insistent pour que nous partions. (Main subject: ils; subordinate subject: nous)

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Confusing the subjunctive with the conditional: Je veux qu'il vient (WRONG) instead of Je veux qu'il vienne. The conditional forms are not used here; use the subjunctive.

- Forgetting the subjunctive after espérer: Espérer usually takes the indicative, not subjunctive. J'espère qu'il vient (indicative: I hope he comes). However, J'espère que tu viennes is borderline and increasingly uncommon.

- Overusing the subjunctive: The indicative is the default mood. English speakers sometimes add the subjunctive where French needs the indicative. Je pense qu'il vient (indicative: I think he is coming) is correct; do not write Je pense qu'il vienne.

- Misplacing ne in negation: Ne...pas goes around the conjugated verb in the subordinate clause. Je veux qu'il ne vienne pas (not qu'il vient pas ne).

- Ignoring nous and vous forms: Many learners use only je/tu/il forms and forget that nous parlions and vous parliez are the correct subjunctive forms for those pronouns.
