All French nouns have two grammatical properties: gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural). These properties are fundamental to French grammar because they affect articles, adjectives, and past participles that accompany the noun. Understanding how to form and recognize these categories is essential for correct French writing and speaking.

## Gender of nouns

- Every French noun is either masculine or feminine. There is no neuter gender in French.
- Gender is a property that must be learned with each noun; it cannot usually be predicted from the word itself.
- Masculine nouns are often preceded by the article le or un: le chien (the dog), un livre (a book), un homme (a man).
- Feminine nouns are often preceded by the article la or une: la maison (the house), une voiture (a car), une femme (a woman).
- A few nouns show gender through their meaning: l'acteur (the male actor) is masculine, but l'actrice (the female actor) is feminine.
- Some common word endings suggest gender. Nouns ending in -e are often feminine: la table (the table), la page (the page). Nouns ending in consonants are often masculine: le parc (the park), le chat (the cat). However, many exceptions exist.

## Number: singular and plural

- French nouns have singular form (one item) and plural form (more than one).
- Singular nouns stand alone and take singular articles and adjectives: un chat (a cat), la maison (the house).
- Plural nouns take plural articles and adjectives: des chats (cats), les maisons (the houses).
- The plural form is usually created by adding -s to the singular noun: le chat becomes les chats, la maison becomes les maisons.
- The plural -s is typically silent when spoken; you recognize plural mainly through the article: un chat versus les chats.

## Rules for forming plurals

- Most nouns add -s to form the plural: le livre (singular) becomes les livres (plural), la table becomes les tables.
- Nouns ending in -au, -eau, or -eu usually add -x instead of -s: le bureau (the desk) becomes les bureaux, le chapeau (the hat) becomes les chapeaux, le jeu (the game) becomes les jeux.
- Nouns ending in -al often change -al to -aux in the plural: le journal (the newspaper) becomes les journaux, l'animal (the animal) becomes les animaux. Common exceptions are le bal (the ball) which becomes les bals and le festival which becomes les festivals.
- Nouns ending in -ail usually add -s: le travail (the work) becomes les travaux. Wait, this is actually an exception: le travail becomes les travaux by changing -ail to -aux.
- Most nouns ending in -ail add -s normally: le détail (the detail) becomes les détails, le mail becomes les mails.
- Some nouns are irregular in the plural: l'œil (the eye) becomes les yeux, le ciel (the sky) becomes les cieux (or les ciels in the meaning of skies).
- A few nouns do not change in the plural; they are called invariable nouns: un euros/des euros.

## Agreement with articles

- Articles must match the gender and number of the noun they introduce.
- Singular masculine: le, un, du (le chien, un chien). Singular feminine: la, une, de la (la maison, une maison).
- Plural regardless of gender: les, des (les chiens, les maisons).
- The indefinite article de or d' is used in negative sentences with plural nouns: Je n'aime pas les chats mais je ne veux pas de chats (I don't like cats but I don't want cats). Compare: Je veux un chat (I want a cat) with Je ne veux pas de chat (I don't want a cat).

## Agreement with adjectives

- Adjectives must match both the gender and number of the noun they modify.
- For a masculine singular noun: un grand chien (a big dog), un jeune homme (a young man).
- For a feminine singular noun: une grande maison (a big house), une jeune femme (a young woman).
- For a plural noun of any gender: des grands chiens (big dogs), des grandes maisons (big houses).
- Most adjectives add -e in the feminine singular: grand becomes grande, but some adjectives like beau, nouveau, and vieux have special feminine forms: le beau garçon (the handsome boy), la belle fille (the beautiful girl); un nouveau restaurant, une nouvelle cuisine.
- Plural adjectives typically add -s: les grands garçons, les grandes filles.

## Common learner mistakes

- Forgetting that all nouns have gender and using the wrong article: saying un table instead of une table, or un chaise instead of une chaise.
- Failing to pluralize the article when pluralizing the noun: writing un chats instead of des chats.
- Confusing the plural of nouns ending in -au or -eau: using des bureaus instead of des bureaux or des chapeaus instead of des chapeaux.
- Not making adjectives agree with feminine or plural nouns: saying un homme grand instead of un grand homme when the adjective precedes, or writing les chats noir instead of les chats noirs.
- Mispronouncing or misunderstanding that the plural -s or -x is silent: students may think les is pronounced differently from le, but the difference is in the article sound, not the noun.
- Mixing gender in compound descriptions: saying l'actrice célèbre (correct: she is a famous actress) but then using masculine adjectives in following sentences.
- Incorrectly forming plurals of words ending in -al: writing les animales instead of les animaux or les journales instead of les journaux.
