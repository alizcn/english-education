French negation requires two elements: the negative adverb "ne" placed before the verb and a second negative word placed after it. This two-part structure is essential to understand because it differs significantly from English, where a single word often negates a sentence.

## Basic Negation with ne...pas

The most common negation in French uses "ne...pas" surrounding the verb. The pattern depends on whether you have an auxiliary verb (avoir, être) or a simple verb in a single tense.

- In simple tenses, place "ne" before the verb and "pas" after it: Je ne vais pas à l'école.
- With compound tenses (passé composé), place "ne" before the auxiliary and "pas" after it: Je n'ai pas mangé.
- The "ne" becomes "n'" before a vowel or silent h: Il n'aime pas les films. Elle n'habite pas ici.
- Common mistakes: learners often forget "ne" entirely, writing "Je vais pas" instead of "Je ne vais pas", or place "pas" in the wrong position.

## Other Common Negation Forms

French offers several other negation structures depending on what you want to negate.

- Ne...jamais (never): Je ne vais jamais au cinéma.
- Ne...plus (no longer): Elle ne travaille plus chez Google.
- Ne...rien (nothing): Je ne vois rien.
- Ne...personne (no one): Je n'aime personne ici.
- Ne...aucun(e) (no, not a single): Je n'ai aucune idée.
- Ne...que (only): Je ne bois que de l'eau. Note: this is restrictive, not truly negative.

## Negation in Questions

When asking a question with inversion, place "ne" before the verb and the second negative element after the inverted subject pronoun.

- Simple inversion: Ne vas-tu pas à la réunion?
- With compound tenses, "ne" comes before the auxiliary: N'as-tu pas vu le film?
- With a noun subject, place the subject after the auxiliary: Ne lit-elle pas ce livre?

## Negation with Object Pronouns

When negating a sentence with direct or indirect object pronouns, the pronouns stay before the verb, and "ne" comes even before the pronoun.

- Je ne le vois pas.
- Elle ne me parle pas.
- Je ne lui ai pas donné le cadeau.
- Common mistake: placing the pronoun after "ne" instead of before the verb.

## Negation in Compound Tenses

In the passé composé and other compound tenses, "ne" comes before the auxiliary (avoir or être) and the second part of the negation typically comes after it.

- Je n'ai pas mangé.
- Il n'est pas allé au parc.
- Elle n'a jamais compris.
- With some negations like "ne...personne", the second element can come at the end: Je n'ai vu personne.

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Forgetting "ne" entirely and saying "Je vais pas" instead of "Je ne vais pas".
- Placing "pas" before the verb or "ne" after it: "Je pas ne vais" is incorrect.
- Forgetting "n'" before vowels and writing "Je ne aime pas" instead of "Je n'aime pas".
- Mixing English and French structures by thinking one negative word is enough.
- Incorrect word order with pronouns, such as "Je pas le vois" instead of "Je ne le vois pas".
- Using "ne...pas" with infinitives incorrectly: the order is "ne pas" before an infinitive, as in "Je préfère ne pas aller."
