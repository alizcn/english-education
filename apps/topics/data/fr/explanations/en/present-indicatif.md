The present indicative (présent de l'indicatif) is the most frequently used tense in French. It expresses what is happening now, what regularly happens, what is true in general, and sometimes even future events that are certain. Mastering this tense is essential because it is the foundation for understanding other verb forms.

## When the Present Indicative is Used

- To describe an action happening right now: Je parle français. (I am speaking French / I speak French.)
- To express habitual or repeated actions: Je vais à l'école tous les jours. (I go to school every day.)
- To state general truths: La terre tourne autour du soleil. (The earth revolves around the sun.)
- To express near future with certainty: Demain, je pars en vacances. (Tomorrow, I am leaving on vacation.)
- In narrative or instructions: Tu ouvres la porte et tu entres dans la pièce. (You open the door and you enter the room.)

## Regular Verbs: The -ER Group

The largest group of regular verbs ends in -er. To form the present, remove the -er ending and add these endings: -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent.

- parler (to speak): je parle, tu parles, il/elle/on parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils/elles parlent
- aimer (to like/love): j'aime, tu aimes, il aime, nous aimons, vous aimez, ils aiment
- marcher (to walk): je marche, tu marches, il marche, nous marchons, vous marchez, ils marchent

Notice that the first three persons singular and the third person plural are pronounced identically, even though they have different spellings.

## Regular Verbs: The -IR and -RE Groups

Verbs ending in -ir drop the -ir and add: -is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent.

- finir (to finish): je finis, tu finis, il finit, nous finissons, vous finissez, ils finissent

Verbs ending in -re drop the -re and add: -s, -s, (nothing), -ons, -ez, -ent.

- vendre (to sell): je vends, tu vends, il vend, nous vendons, vous vendez, ils vendent
- attendre (to wait): j'attends, tu attends, il attend, nous attendons, vous attendez, ils attendent

## Common Irregular Verbs

Some of the most frequently used verbs are irregular in the present tense. These must be memorized.

- être (to be): je suis, tu es, il est, nous sommes, vous êtes, ils sont
- avoir (to have): j'ai, tu as, il a, nous avons, vous avez, ils ont
- aller (to go): je vais, tu vas, il va, nous allons, vous allez, ils vont
- faire (to do/make): je fais, tu fais, il fait, nous faisons, vous faites, ils font
- pouvoir (can/to be able): je peux, tu peux, il peut, nous pouvons, vous pouvez, ils peuvent
- vouloir (to want): je veux, tu veux, il veut, nous voulons, vous voulez, ils veulent
- devoir (must/to have to): je dois, tu dois, il doit, nous devons, vous devez, ils doivent
- savoir (to know): je sais, tu sais, il sait, nous savons, vous savez, ils savent

## Negative and Question Forms

To form the negative, place ne before the verb and pas (or another negative word) after it. If the verb begins with a vowel, ne becomes n'.

- Je ne parle pas français. (I do not speak French.)
- Elle n'aime pas les épinards. (She does not like spinach.)
- Ils ne vont pas à la plage. (They are not going to the beach.)

For questions, you have three main options. The simplest is to use intonation: Tu parles français? The second is to invert the verb and subject: Parles-tu français? The third is to use est-ce que: Est-ce que tu parles français?

- Mangez-vous de la pizza? (Do you eat pizza?)
- Est-ce que vous finissez vos devoirs? (Are you finishing your homework?)
- Ils reviennent demain? (Are they coming back tomorrow?)

## Time Expressions and Frequency Markers

Certain words and phrases commonly appear with the present tense to indicate when or how often something happens.

- maintenant (now): Je travaille maintenant. (I am working now.)
- aujourd'hui (today): Aujourd'hui, je suis heureux. (Today, I am happy.)
- en ce moment (at the moment): En ce moment, il pleut. (At the moment, it is raining.)
- toujours (always): Je suis toujours fatigué. (I am always tired.)
- souvent (often): Elle lit souvent. (She often reads.)
- parfois (sometimes): Parfois, nous allons au cinéma. (Sometimes, we go to the movies.)
- rarement (rarely): Je sors rarement le soir. (I rarely go out in the evening.)
- jamais (never): Ils ne mangent jamais de viande. (They never eat meat.)
- chaque jour / tous les jours (every day): Chaque jour, il prend le bus. (Every day, he takes the bus.)

## Common Mistakes Learners Make

A frequent error is forgetting that the present indicative can express the near future in French. English speakers often try to use a future tense instead: Demain, je vais au cinéma. (Tomorrow, I am going to the movies.) This is correct in French even though English would say "I will go."

Another mistake is incorrect verb endings. Learners sometimes apply -ER endings to -IR verbs: je finire instead of je finis. Remember that -IR verbs use the -issons and -issez pattern for nous and vous.

Pronunciation can also mislead you. Because most present-tense forms sound the same, learners sometimes misspell them. All singular forms of parler (je parle, tu parles, il parle) sound identical, but they must be spelled differently.

A third error involves irregular verbs. Students sometimes try to regularize them. Être and avoir, being used so frequently, deserve extra attention: je suis (not je somme) and j'ai (not j'aie).

Finally, learners forget the negation ne. Saying Je pas aime is incomplete; you must say Je n'aime pas. This applies to all verbs without exception.
