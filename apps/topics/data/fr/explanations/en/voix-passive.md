The passive voice in French is used to shift focus from the doer of an action to the action itself and its recipient. Instead of saying who performs the action, you emphasize what happens. This construction is called voix passive and uses the auxiliary verb être plus the past participle of the main verb.

## When the passive voice is used

The passive voice is appropriate when the agent (the person doing the action) is unknown, unimportant, or already clear from context. It is also used in formal writing, scientific texts, and news reporting. In everyday speech, French speakers use it much less frequently than English speakers do. The passive voice focuses attention on the object of the action rather than the subject.

- Use it when you want to emphasize the action or its result rather than who performs it.
- Use it in formal or written contexts.
- Avoid overusing it in spoken French, as native speakers often prefer active constructions.

## Formation of the positive passive voice

The passive voice is constructed with the auxiliary verb être conjugated in the appropriate tense, followed by the past participle of the main verb. The past participle must always agree in gender and number with the subject. Basic structure: subject + être (conjugated) + past participle + optional par-phrase.

Simple present passive examples:
- Le chat est nourri par Marie. (The cat is fed by Marie.)
- Les lettres sont écrites par l'employée. (The letters are written by the employee.)
- Je suis invité à la fête. (I am invited to the party.)

Passé composé examples:
- Le rapport a été rédigé hier. (The report was written yesterday.)
- Les colis ont été livrés ce matin. (The packages were delivered this morning.)
- Elles ont été félicitées pour leur travail. (They were congratulated for their work.)

Imparfait examples:
- La maison était construite lentement. (The house was being built slowly.)
- Les enfants étaient surveillés constamment. (The children were constantly watched.)

## Negative forms of the passive voice

To make a passive construction negative, place ne before the auxiliary être and pas after it, following standard French negation rules. The passive voice structure otherwise remains unchanged.

- Le chat n'est pas nourri par Marie. (The cat is not fed by Marie.)
- Les lettres ne sont pas écrites par l'employée. (The letters are not written by the employee.)
- Je ne suis pas invité à la fête. (I am not invited to the party.)

In the passé composé:
- Le rapport n'a pas été rédigé hier. (The report was not written yesterday.)
- Les colis n'ont pas été livrés ce matin. (The packages were not delivered this morning.)

With other negation structures:
- Aucun élève n'a été accepté. (No student was accepted.)
- Le travail ne sera jamais terminé. (The work will never be finished.)

## Questions in the passive voice

Questions can be formed in three ways: inversion, est-ce que, or intonation. The passive voice itself does not change structurally, but word order may shift when using inversion.

Using inversion:
- Le chat est-il nourri par Marie? (Is the cat fed by Marie?)
- Les lettres ont-elles été écrites par l'employée? (Were the letters written by the employee?)
- Êtes-vous invité à la fête? (Are you invited to the party?)

Using est-ce que:
- Est-ce que le chat est nourri par Marie? (Is the cat fed by Marie?)
- Est-ce que les lettres ont été écrites par l'employée? (Were the letters written by the employee?)

Using intonation (spoken French):
- Le chat est nourri par Marie? (The cat is fed by Marie?)
- Les lettres ont été écrites par l'employée? (The letters were written by the employee?)

## Agreement rules for the past participle

This is the most important morphological rule: the past participle in the passive voice must always agree in gender and number with the subject of the sentence. This agreement is mandatory and visible in writing with regular verbs.

Masculine singular: Le garçon est invité. (The boy is invited.)
Feminine singular: La fille est invitée. (The girl is invited.)
Masculine plural: Les garçons sont invités. (The boys are invited.)
Feminine plural: Les filles sont invitées. (The girls are invited.)

With irregular past participles, agreement is still mandatory but may be less visible in pronunciation:
- Les portes sont ouvertes. (The doors are opened.)
- Le livre est écrit. (The book is written.)
- La chanson est chantée. (The song is sung.)

The optional par-phrase does not affect agreement:
- Les lettres sont écrites. (The letters are written.)
- Les lettres sont écrites par Jean. (The letters are written by Jean.)

## Common mistakes learners make

The biggest error is forgetting to agree the past participle with the subject. This agreement is mandatory in the passive voice, unlike in the passé composé with avoir where agreement is sometimes optional.

Incorrect: La fille est invite à la fête. (missing feminine -e)
Correct: La fille est invitée à la fête. (The girl is invited to the party.)

Incorrect: Les garçons ont été reçu par le directeur. (missing plural -s)
Correct: Les garçons ont été reçus par le directeur. (The boys were received by the principal.)

Another mistake is overusing the passive voice. Learners sometimes force passive constructions where active voice sounds more natural. Remember that native speakers use the passive voice less in everyday French than in English.

Unnatural passive: Mon ami m'a été présenté par Marie.
More natural active: Marie m'a présenté son ami. (Marie introduced her friend to me.)

Learners also confuse the par agent phrase with other prepositional phrases. Only par introduces the true agent:
- La pièce est éclairée par le soleil. (The room is lit by the sun. - true agent with par)
- La pièce est pleine de livres. (The room is full of books. - descriptive phrase with de, not passive)

## Typical contexts and time expressions

The passive voice appears frequently in news reports, official instructions, academic writing, and formal communications. Time expressions work normally within passive constructions.

News context:
- Le criminel a été arrêté ce matin. (The criminal was arrested this morning.)
- L'accident a été causé par une défaillance mécanique. (The accident was caused by mechanical failure.)

Formal instructions or policies:
- Les candidats doivent être notifiés avant le 31 décembre. (Candidates must be notified before December 31.)
- Le rapport sera transmis à la direction demain. (The report will be transmitted to management tomorrow.)

Habitual or general statements:
- Les enfants sont généralement supervisés à l'école. (Children are generally supervised at school.)
- Les devoirs sont collectés le vendredi. (Homework is collected on Friday.)
- Les résultats seront annoncés prochainement. (Results will be announced soon.)
