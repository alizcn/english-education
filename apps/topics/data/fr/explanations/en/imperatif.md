The Impératif (Imperative) is the mood used to give commands, make requests, or issue invitations. It addresses the person or people you are speaking to directly, without using the subject pronoun. The imperative exists only for three subject forms: tu (one person, informal), nous (let's do something), and vous (multiple people or formal singular).

## When to Use the Imperative

- Giving direct commands or orders
- Making polite requests or suggestions
- Offering invitations
- Giving instructions or recipes

Descends les escaliers. (Come down the stairs.)

Passe-moi le sel, s'il te plaît. (Pass me the salt, please.)

Venez nous voir demain. (Come see us tomorrow.)

## Formation of Positive Commands

For regular -er verbs like parler (to speak), the tu and vous forms match the present tense, but the tu form drops the final -s:

tu: parle

nous: parlons

vous: parlez

For regular -ir verbs like finir (to finish) and -re verbs like vendre (to sell), the forms are identical to their present tense:

tu: finis / vends

nous: finissons / vendons

vous: finissez / vendez

Parle français! (Speak French!)

Finis ton devoir. (Finish your homework.)

Vendez votre maison. (Sell your house.)

## Spelling Rules and Exceptions

The -er verb rule about dropping -s in the tu form has one important exception: keep the -s when the verb is followed by the pronouns y or en. This keeps the sentence pronounceable.

Pense à ton examen. (Think of your exam.)

But: Penses-y. (Think about it.)

Va à l'école. (Go to school.)

But: Vas-y. (Go there.)

Several common verbs are irregular in the imperative: avoir (aie, ayons, ayez), être (sois, soyons, soyez), aller (va, allons, allez), and savoir (sache, sachons, sachez). Many of these match the subjunctive form.

Sois heureux! (Be happy!)

Ayons confiance. (Let's have confidence.)

Va tout droit. (Go straight ahead.)

## Negative Commands and Reflexive Verbs

To negate a command, place ne before the verb and pas (or autre negation) after it, keeping the same verb form. Object pronouns and reflexive pronouns stay before the verb in negatives.

Ne parle pas en classe. (Don't speak in class.)

N'allez pas là-bas. (Don't go there.)

For reflexive verbs, the pronoun attaches to the verb with a hyphen in positive commands but stays before the verb in negatives.

Lève-toi! (Get up!)

Asseyons-nous. (Let's sit down.)

But: Ne te lève pas. (Don't get up.)

Ne nous asseyons pas. (Let's not sit down.)

## Questions with the Imperative

Questions are rarely formed with the imperative mood because you are giving a command, not asking a question. If you soften a request, use the conditional instead (Pourrais-tu m'aider?) or the question form of the present tense (Peux-tu m'aider?).

## Common Mistakes English Speakers Make

Including the subject pronoun is a frequent error: do not write Tu parle! but simply Parle! The subject is implied in the verb form itself.

Forgetting the -s drop in -er verbs causes problems: use parle, never parles, except when followed by y or en. Many learners think the present tense rule applies to the imperative without exception.

Confusing imperative with the subjunctive mood leads to wrong forms: while some forms overlap (especially with irregular verbs), they are not interchangeable. The imperative is for direct commands; the subjunctive follows expressions of doubt, emotion, or necessity.

Using vous for one person when tu is appropriate, or vice versa: remember that vous is formal singular or any plural, while tu is informal singular. A child or close friend takes tu; a stranger or authority figure takes vous.

Incorrectly placing object pronouns: after affirmative imperatives, pronouns attach with hyphens (Dis-moi, Donne-le-lui), but in negatives they come before the verb (Ne me dis pas, Ne le lui donne pas).
