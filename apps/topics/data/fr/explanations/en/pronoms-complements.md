Complement pronouns in French (pronoms compléments) replace nouns that function as the direct or indirect objects of a verb. There are two types: direct object pronouns (COD - complément d'objet direct), which replace nouns receiving the action directly, and indirect object pronouns (COI - complément d'objet indirect), which replace nouns receiving the action indirectly through a preposition.

## Direct Object Pronouns (COD)

Direct object pronouns replace the person or thing that directly receives the verb's action, answering "who" or "what" (whom/what).

- The six direct object pronouns are: me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les.
- le and la elide to l' before a vowel or mute h: Je l'aime (I love him/her), Je l'appelle (I call him/her), Je l'habille (I dress him/her).
- Without elision before consonants: Je le vois (I see him), Je la regarde (I watch her).
- Examples: Tu vois Marie? Oui, je la vois (Do you see Marie? Yes, I see her). Elle achète des livres? Oui, elle les achète (Does she buy books? Yes, she buys them).

## Indirect Object Pronouns (COI)

Indirect object pronouns replace nouns that receive the action indirectly, typically related to prepositions like "à" (to, for). They answer "to whom" or "for whom."

- The six indirect object pronouns are: me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur.
- Note that me, te, nous, vous function as both direct and indirect pronouns depending on the verb.
- Examples: Tu parles à Pierre? Oui, je lui parle (Do you talk to Pierre? Yes, I talk to him). Elle répond aux enfants? Oui, elle leur répond (Does she answer the children? Yes, she answers them).
- Common verbs taking COI: parler à, répondre à, écrire à, téléphoner à, donner à, demander à, dire à, obéir à.

## Placement in Positive, Negative, and Question Forms

Complement pronouns always come immediately before the conjugated verb in the sentence.

- Positive: Je le vois (I see him), Nous leur parlons (We talk to them).
- Negative: The pronoun remains before the verb between the two negative particles: Je ne le vois pas (I don't see him), Elle ne leur parle pas (She doesn't talk to them).
- Inverted questions: The pronoun precedes the conjugated verb, which is then followed by the hyphen and subject pronoun: Le vois-tu? (Do you see him?), Lui parlez-vous? (Do you talk to him?).
- In compound tenses, the pronoun comes before the auxiliary verb: Je l'ai vu (I saw him), Je ne l'ai pas vu (I didn't see him), Je lui ai parlé (I talked to him).

## Distinguishing COD from COI

A common challenge is identifying whether a verb takes a direct or indirect object.

- Test with questions: Ask "whom/what" for COD and "to/for whom" for COI. Je vois Maria (I see whom? → Je la vois, COD). Je parle à Maria (I speak to whom? → Je lui parle, COI).
- Beware of English misleading you: Écouter (to listen to) is COD in French, so Je l'écoute (I listen to him), even though English uses the preposition "to." Similarly, Regarder (to look at) is COD: Je le regarde (I look at him).
- Verbs like obéir à, parler à, répondre à, téléphoner à, and écrire à always take COI: Je lui obéis (I obey him), Je lui écris (I write to him).

## Common Mistakes Learners Make

- Confusing le/la with lui: Je lui regarde should be Je le regarde (regarder takes COD). Je l'obéis should be Je lui obéis (obéir à takes COI). This is the most frequent error.
- Omitting elision: Je le aime should be Je l'aime. Je la appelle should be Je l'appelle.
- Misplacing the pronoun: Je vois le should be Je le vois. The pronoun must come before the verb, not after it.
- Wrong negative structure: Je ne vois pas le should be Je ne le vois pas. The pronoun goes between ne and the verb.
- Confusing English syntax: Since English often uses prepositions with these verbs (listen to, look at, speak to), learners sometimes add prepositions in French where they don't belong: Je lui écoute should be Je l'écoute. Je lui regarde should be Je le regarde.
