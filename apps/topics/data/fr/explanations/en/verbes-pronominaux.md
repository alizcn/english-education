Reflexive verbs in French are verbs where the action is performed by the subject on themselves. They use reflexive pronouns (me, te, se, nous, vous) positioned before the verb. Understanding when and how to use these verbs correctly is essential for speaking natural French.

## What Are Reflexive Verbs?

A reflexive verb shows that the subject acts upon themselves. The reflexive pronoun indicates this relationship.

- Je me lave. (I wash myself.)
- Il se réveille à sept heures. (He wakes himself up at seven o'clock.)
- Nous nous amusons. (We are having fun - literally, amusing ourselves.)

Some French reflexive verbs have no reflexive equivalent in English; they are simply used as reflexive in French.

- Je m'en vais. (I am leaving.)
- Elle se souvient de son enfance. (She remembers her childhood.)

## Forming the Reflexive Verb: Positive

The reflexive pronoun always precedes the verb. Here is the pattern with the present tense:

- je me lève (I get up)
- tu te lèves (you get up)
- il/elle/on se lève (he/she/one gets up)
- nous nous levons (we get up)
- vous vous levez (you all get up)
- ils/elles se lèvent (they get up)

This same pronoun placement applies to all tenses and moods. In compound tenses, the reflexive pronoun stays before the auxiliary verb:

- Je me suis levé. (I got up.)
- Nous nous sommes amusés. (We had fun.)

## Negative and Interrogative Forms

Negation uses the standard ne...pas pattern, with the reflexive pronoun still placed before the verb:

- Je ne me lève pas. (I do not get up.)
- Il ne s'endort jamais avant minuit. (He never falls asleep before midnight.)

For questions, inversion applies to the subject pronoun and auxiliary (in compound tenses), while the reflexive pronoun remains before the verb:

- Te lèves-tu tôt? (Do you get up early?)
- S'est-il réveillé? (Did he wake up?)
- Vous vous amusez bien? (Are you having fun? - informal question with rising intonation)

With the est-ce que structure, the reflexive pronoun stays in its normal position:

- Est-ce que tu te lèves tôt? (Do you get up early?)
- Est-ce qu'elle se souvient? (Does she remember?)

## Reflexive Pronoun Agreement in Compound Tenses

Reflexive verbs always use the auxiliary être in compound tenses. The past participle must agree with the reflexive pronoun when it is the direct object:

- Elle s'est levée. (She got up. - feminine singular agreement)
- Ils se sont réveillés. (They woke up. - masculine plural agreement)
- Elles se sont amusées. (They had fun. - feminine plural agreement)

However, if the reflexive pronoun is an indirect object, agreement does not occur:

- Elles se sont écrit des lettres. (They wrote letters to each other. - no agreement; se is indirect object)

## Common Reflexive Verbs and Their Meanings

- s'appeler: to be called, to call oneself (Je m'appelle Marie.)
- se réveiller: to wake up (Tu te réveilles tard le samedi.)
- se lever: to get up, to stand up (Il se lève à six heures.)
- se laver: to wash oneself (Nous nous lavons les mains.)
- s'habiller: to get dressed (Elles s'habillent rapidement.)
- s'endormir: to fall asleep (L'enfant s'endort facilement.)
- se coucher: to go to bed, to lie down (Vous vous couchez tôt?)
- s'amuser: to have fun (Ils s'amusent au parc.)
- se souvenir: to remember (Tu te souviens de ce jour?)
- se tromper: to be mistaken (Je me suis trompée dans mon calcul.)
- se dépêcher: to hurry (Dépêche-toi! We're late.)
- se rencontrer: to meet each other (Où vous êtes-vous rencontrés?)

## Common Learner Mistakes

Many learners forget to include the reflexive pronoun entirely, or place it incorrectly:

- WRONG: Il lève à sept heures.
- CORRECT: Il se lève à sept heures.

Learners sometimes confuse the reflexive pronoun with the direct object pronoun:

- WRONG: Je me vois demain.
- CORRECT: Je vous vois demain. (I see you tomorrow. - vous is the direct object pronoun here, not reflexive)
- OR: Je me vois dans le miroir. (I see myself in the mirror. - reflexive)

Another frequent error is forgetting to use être as the auxiliary in the passé composé:

- WRONG: J'ai me levé.
- CORRECT: Je me suis levé.

Learners sometimes fail to make the past participle agree when necessary:

- WRONG: Elles se sont levé. (They got up.)
- CORRECT: Elles se sont levées.

Finally, some learners incorrectly think reflexive pronouns can be omitted in certain contexts, when in French they must always appear:

- WRONG: Après, l'enfant endort.
- CORRECT: Après, l'enfant s'endort. (Afterward, the child falls asleep.)
