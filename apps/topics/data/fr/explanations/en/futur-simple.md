Le futur simple (the simple future tense) is used to express actions that will happen at a specific point in the future. Unlike the near future constructed with aller, the futur simple is more formal and literary, widely used in French for definite events, plans, and predictions. It is one of the most important ways to talk about what will happen.

## When to use the futur simple

- Use it for definite future plans or actions: Je partirai demain. (I will leave tomorrow.)
- Use it for predictions about the future: Il fera beau. (It will be nice weather.)
- Use it in formal, written, or literary contexts where you need a more serious tone: Vous recevrez une réponse bientôt. (You will receive an answer soon.)
- Use it after time conjunctions like "quand" and "si" in the main clause: Quand tu reviendras, nous ferons la fête. (When you come back, we will have a party.)

## How to form the positive futur simple

- For regular verbs, take the entire infinitive and add the future endings: -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont
- With first group verbs (infinitive ending in -er): Je parlerai, tu parleras, il parlera, nous parlerons, vous parlerez, ils parleront
- With second group verbs (infinitive ending in -ir): Je finirai, tu finiras, elle finira, nous finirons, vous finirez, elles finiront
- With third group verbs (infinitive ending in -re): Je vendrai, tu vendras, on vendra, nous vendrons, vous vendrez, ils vendront

## Irregular verbs in the futur simple

- Many common verbs have irregular future stems. Learn these carefully: Avoir (j'aurai), Être (je serai), Aller (j'irai), Faire (je ferai), Venir (je viendrai), Devoir (je devrai), Pouvoir (je pourrai), Vouloir (je voudrai), Tenir (je tiendrai), Voir (je verrai), Savoir (je saurai)
- The irregular stem remains the same for all persons: Je ferai, tu feras, il fera, nous ferons, vous ferez, ils feront

## Questions and negatives

- To form questions, use inversion with the verb: Parlerez-vous français? (Will you speak French?) or use est-ce que: Est-ce que vous parlerez français?
- To form negatives, place ne before the verb and pas after: Je ne parlerai pas. (I will not speak.)
- In negative questions, both structures work: Ne parlerez-vous pas? or Est-ce que tu ne parleras pas? (Will you not speak?)

## Time expressions commonly used with futur simple

- Demain (tomorrow), la semaine prochaine (next week), le mois prochain (next month), l'année prochaine (next year), dans trois jours (in three days), bientôt (soon), plus tard (later), un jour (one day)
- Examples: Je t'appellerai demain. (I will call you tomorrow.) Nous irons à la plage la semaine prochaine. (We will go to the beach next week.)

## Common mistakes learners make

- Confusing the futur simple with aller plus infinitive (the near future): Je vais parler means I am going to speak (near future), while Je parlerai means I will speak (simple future). Both are correct but have different nuances and registers.
- Using the wrong stem for irregular verbs: Students often forget to memorize irregular stems and incorrectly say Je vais faire instead of Je ferai, or Je peux instead of Je pourrai.
- Placing the negative incorrectly: The structure must be ne + verb + pas. Never say Je pas parlerai or Je ne pas parlerai.
- Forgetting to conjugate: Remember that the future ending agrees with the subject: Je parlerai (not Je parler), tu parleras (not tu parler).
