Das Present Perfect ist eine englische Zeitform, die verwendet wird, um Handlungen auszudrücken, die in der Vergangenheit begonnen haben oder stattgefunden haben, aber eine Verbindung zur Gegenwart besitzen. Diese Zeitform kombiniert das Präsens des Hilfsverbs "have" oder "has" mit dem Past Participle des Hauptverbs.

## Wann verwendet man das Present Perfect

Das Present Perfect beschreibt:
- Erfahrungen und Tätigkeiten, die irgendwann in der Vergangenheit stattgefunden haben: I have visited Paris.
- Handlungen, die in der Vergangenheit begannen und bis zur Gegenwart andauern: She has lived in Berlin for ten years.
- Ergebnisse von Handlungen, deren Effekt bis jetzt spürbar ist: He has finished his homework, so he can go out.
- Handlungen, die gerade abgeschlossen wurden: They have just arrived at the station.

## Bildung des Present Perfect

Das Present Perfect wird mit dem Präsens von "have" oder "has" plus dem Past Participle gebildet.

Affirmativ:
- I have worked.
- She has studied.
- We have learned.

Negativ: Man setzt "not" nach "have" oder "has":
- I have not worked.
- She has not studied.
- They have not learned.

Oder in der Kurzform:
- I haven't worked.
- She hasn't studied.
- They haven't learned.

Fragen: Das Hilfsverb steht vor dem Subjekt:
- Have you worked?
- Has she studied?
- Have they learned?

## Past Participles: Regelmäßige und unregelmäßige Formen

Regelmäßige Verben bilden das Past Participle durch Addition von "-ed":
- work → worked
- play → played
- study → studied

Beachten Sie Schreibregeln bei regulären Verben:
- Verben, die auf -e enden, nehmen nur "-d": love → loved, like → liked
- Einsilbige Verben mit Vokal und Konsonant verdoppeln den Konsonanten: stop → stopped, plan → planned
- Verben auf -y wechseln das y zu i: study → studied, cry → cried

Unregelmäßige Verben haben eigene Formen, die gelernt werden müssen:
- be → been (I have been there.)
- go → gone (She has gone to school.)
- do → done (Have you done your homework?)
- eat → eaten (We have eaten dinner.)
- see → seen (They have seen the film.)
- have → had (I have had enough.)
- come → come (He has come home.)

## Typische Zeitausdrücke und Signalwörter

Bestimmte Ausdrücke signalisieren das Present Perfect:
- Just: He has just finished.
- Already: She has already left.
- Yet: Have you finished yet?
- For: I have worked here for five years.
- Since: We have known each other since 2015.
- Ever: Have you ever been to America?
- Never: I have never seen that before.
- Lately: They have been very busy lately.
- Recently: She has recently moved to London.

Das Present Perfect wird häufig ohne spezifische Zeitangabe verwendet, wenn die Zeit nicht wichtig ist: I have read that book.

## Häufige Fehler von Lernenden

Fehler 1: Verwirrung mit dem Simple Past
- Falsch: I have went to the cinema yesterday.
- Richtig: I went to the cinema yesterday. (Weil "yesterday" ein konkreter Zeitpunkt ist)
- Oder: I have been to the cinema. (Ohne Zeitangabe)

Fehler 2: Falsches Hilfsverb
- Falsch: She do finished her work.
- Richtig: She has finished her work.

Fehler 3: Unregelmäßige Verben ignorieren
- Falsch: He has goed to school.
- Richtig: He has gone to school.

Fehler 4: Past Participle vergessen
- Falsch: I have study English for two years.
- Richtig: I have studied English for two years.

Fehler 5: "For" und "since" verwechseln
- Falsch: I have lived here since five years.
- Richtig: I have lived here for five years.
- Richtig: I have lived here since 2020.

## Present Perfect Continuous

Das Present Perfect Continuous (have/has been + -ing) betont die Dauer einer Handlung:
- I have been working on this project for three hours.
- She has been waiting for the bus since noon.

Der Unterschied zum Present Perfect:
- She has finished the report. (Resultat ist wichtig)
- She has been writing the report. (Die Aktivität und Dauer sind wichtig)
