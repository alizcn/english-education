Das Present Perfect Continuous wird verwendet, um eine Aktivität zu beschreiben, die in der Vergangenheit begonnen hat und bis zur Gegenwart andauert oder gerade eben beendet wurde. Der Fokus liegt auf der Dauer und dem fortlaufenden Prozess einer Handlung, nicht auf dem Ergebnis.

## Verwendung des Present Perfect Continuous

- Handlungen, die in der Vergangenheit begonnen haben und noch immer andauern: I have been living in Berlin since 2018.
- Aktivitäten, die gerade eben beendet wurden und deren Effekt noch spürbar ist: I have been exercising, so I am tired.
- Betonung der Dauer statt des Ergebnisses einer Handlung.
- Häufig bei Fragen mit "How long...?": How long have you been studying English?

## Bildung des Present Perfect Continuous

Positive Form: have oder has + been + Verb mit -ing Endung

- I have been working since this morning.
- She has been teaching for ten years.
- We have been waiting for an hour.

Negative Form: have oder has + not + been + Verb mit -ing Endung

- I have not been working today.
- He has not been helping with the project.
- They have not been sleeping well recently.

Frageform: have oder has + Subjekt + been + Verb mit -ing Endung

- Have you been studying hard?
- Has she been working on the assignment?
- How long have they been living together?

## Zeitausdrücke und Marker

- For + Zeitdauer: for three hours, for two weeks, for a year, for months
- Since + Zeitpunkt: since 2020, since Monday, since this morning, since I arrived
- How long + Frage: How long have you been here?
- All day, all morning, all week: I have been cleaning all morning.
- Recently, lately: She has been feeling stressed lately.

Beispiele: I have been reading for an hour. She has been learning piano since she was five. How long have they been waiting?

## Besonderheiten bei der -ing Form

- Verben ohne auslautendes e: work → working, teach → teaching, help → helping
- Verben mit auslautendem e: Das e fällt weg: live → living, come → coming, move → moving
- Verben mit y am Ende: Das y wird zu i: study → studying, cry → crying, try → trying
- Einkonsotantigung bei kurzen Vokalen: Konsonant wird verdoppelt: run → running, sit → sitting, stop → stopping, plan → planning
- Verben mit ie: die → dying, lie → lying

## Häufige Fehler von Lernenden

- Fehler: I am working here since five years. Richtig: I have been working here for five years. ("For" steht bei Dauer, "since" bei Zeitpunkt)
- Fehler: She is studying English for two years. Richtig: She has been studying English for two years.
- Fehler: I have working here. Richtig: I have been working here. (Das Wort "been" darf nicht weggelassen werden)
- Fehler: They are being at home since Monday. Richtig: They have been at home since Monday. ("Be" kann kein Present Perfect Continuous bilden)
- Verwechslung mit Simple Past: I worked there for five years (abgeschlossene Tätigkeit in der Vergangenheit) statt I have been working there for five years (Tätigkeit bis jetzt).

## Unterschied zum Present Perfect Simple

- Present Perfect Continuous betont die Dauer und den Prozess: I have been reading for two hours.
- Present Perfect Simple betont das Ergebnis oder die Vollendung: I have read the book.
- How many pages have you read? (Ergebnis, Simple) vs. How long have you been reading? (Dauer, Continuous)
- She has been cooking. (Der Prozess war gerade eben aktiv) vs. She has cooked dinner. (Das Ergebnis liegt vor)
- Present Perfect Continuous mit Verben wie sit, wait, live, work: I have been waiting for him. (Simple ist auch möglich: I have waited for him, aber Continuous betont die Dauer mehr)
