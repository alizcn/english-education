The past perfect is a verb tense that expresses an action that happened before another action in the past. It is formed with had and the past participle. This tense helps you show the order of two or more events that both occurred in the past.

## When to Use the Past Perfect

- Use the past perfect to show that one action happened before another action in the past.
- The action that happened first uses the past perfect; the action that happened second uses the simple past.
- You use this tense when you want to make it clear which event happened first and which happened second.

Example: When I arrived at the cinema, the film had already started. (The film started first, then I arrived.)

Example: She had finished her homework before her mother came home.

Example: They had never seen a desert before they moved to Arizona.

## How to Form the Affirmative

- The past perfect uses two parts: had (the auxiliary verb) and the past participle of the main verb.
- All subjects use had, not has or have. Subject pronouns do not change the form of had.
- I had worked, you had worked, he had worked, she had worked, it had worked, we had worked, they had worked.

- Regular past participles end in -ed: work becomes worked, play becomes played, watch becomes watched.
- Irregular past participles do not follow this pattern: go becomes gone, eat becomes eaten, see becomes seen, break becomes broken.

Example: I had already called the doctor when she finally arrived.

Example: The students had studied the chapter before the test.

Example: He had written three emails by lunch time.

## Negative and Question Forms

- To make a negative statement, add not after had. You can also use the contraction hadn't instead of had not.
- I had not finished the project. Or: I hadn't finished the project.
- They had not arrived when we left. Or: They hadn't arrived when we left.

- To ask a question, put had at the beginning of the sentence before the subject.
- Had you seen that film before? (Yes, I had. No, I hadn't.)
- Had she completed the assignment? (Yes, she had. No, she hadn't.)

Example: I hadn't realized how late it was.

Example: Had they lived in London before moving to Manchester?

Example: She hadn't expected such a large crowd.

## Time Markers and Expressions

- Words and phrases that often signal the past perfect include: before, after, by the time, by then, already, just, previously, earlier, and when.
- These expressions help show the sequence and timing of past events.

Example: By the time the police arrived, the thief had escaped.

Example: After he had eaten dinner, he watched television.

Example: She had already left when I called.

Example: Just before the meeting started, I had realized I had forgotten the documents.

Example: When they reached the train station, the train had already departed.

## Common Mistakes Learners Make

- Mistake: Using the past simple for both actions instead of showing which one happened first. Wrong: When I arrived, the film started. Right: When I arrived, the film had started.

- Mistake: Using have instead of had. Wrong: I have seen that film before I moved here. Right: I had seen that film before I moved there.

- Mistake: Using the wrong past participle form, especially with irregular verbs. Wrong: He had go to the store before lunch. Right: He had gone to the store before lunch.

- Mistake: Overusing the past perfect when only the simple past is needed. Wrong: I had worked at that company for five years. Right: I worked at that company for five years. (Only use past perfect if another past action is mentioned.)

- Mistake: Mixing up the word order in questions. Wrong: They had seen the movie had? Right: Had they seen the movie?

## Past Perfect Compared to Simple Past

- Use the simple past for a single action in the past or for an action that happened second (more recently).
- Use the past perfect for an action that happened first (further back in the past).

Example: She ate breakfast and then she went to work. (Both past actions are presented in time order, so simple past works for both.)

Example: By the time she arrived at work, she had eaten breakfast. (Eating breakfast happened first.)

Example: I saw the movie last week, but I had seen it before in 2020. (The 2020 viewing happened first, so it takes past perfect.)
