The Past Simple is the most basic past tense in English. It describes completed actions or states that happened at a specific time in the past. You use it constantly when telling stories, describing events, or talking about your experience.

## When to Use the Past Simple

- Use it for actions that started and finished at a definite time in the past: I visited Paris last summer. She finished her homework yesterday.
- Use it for a series of completed actions in the past: I woke up, drank coffee, and went to work.
- Use it for past states or situations that are now finished: He lived in London for ten years. The weather was cold all winter.
- Use it for repeated past habits: I played tennis every weekend when I was younger.
- Use it for past facts or historical events: The First World War ended in 1918. Shakespeare died in 1616.

## Regular Verbs: How to Form Them

- Regular verbs add -ed to the base form: work becomes worked, walk becomes walked, study becomes studied.
- Positive: Subject + past tense verb. Example: She worked late yesterday. They played football in the park.
- Negative: Subject + did not (didn't) + base form. Example: I didn't finish my project. He didn't see the movie.
- Question: Did + subject + base form. Example: Did you visit your family? Did they enjoy the concert?

## Irregular Verbs

- English has many irregular verbs that change their form in ways you must memorize: go becomes went, see becomes saw, eat becomes ate.
- Common irregular verbs include: be (was/were), have (had), do (did), say (said), get (got), make (made), give (gave), find (found), tell (told), know (knew).
- Irregular verbs use the same negative and question patterns as regular verbs: I didn't go to school. Did you know about this?
- Examples in sentences: She went to Paris. I had a good time. They did their homework. We saw the game on television.

## Spelling Rules for Regular Verbs

- For verbs ending in consonant + y, change the y to i and add -ed: study becomes studied, cry becomes cried, try becomes tried.
- For verbs ending in e, just add -d: love becomes loved, live becomes lived, use becomes used.
- For verbs ending in a single vowel + single consonant, double the final consonant before adding -ed: stop becomes stopped, plan becomes planned, prefer becomes preferred.
- For most other verbs, simply add -ed: walk becomes walked, play becomes played, want becomes wanted.

## Time Expressions with Past Simple

- Use these time markers to show when something happened: yesterday, last week, last month, last year, three days ago, ten years ago, in 1995.
- Use ago after a time expression: I started learning English two years ago. He visited us five days ago.
- Use last with days, weeks, months, or years: Last Monday was cold. I saw her last summer.
- These expressions help you understand if a sentence should use Past Simple: When you see yesterday or last month, Past Simple is usually correct.

## Common Mistakes Learners Make

- Mistake: Using did with the base form in positive sentences. Wrong: I did go to the market. Correct: I went to the market.
- Mistake: Forgetting to use did in questions. Wrong: You went to school? Correct: Did you go to school?
- Mistake: Using -ed with irregular verbs. Wrong: I goed. I sayed. Correct: I went. I said.
- Mistake: Mixing Past Simple and Present Perfect. Wrong: I lived in this city since 2020. Correct: I have lived in this city since 2020. (Or: I lived in this city in 2020.)
- Mistake: Saying "I didn't went." Remember: Use didn't plus the base form, never the past tense form.
- Mistake: Forgetting that was and were are the only past forms of be. You use them even though they look different from regular -ed verbs.
