"Be going to" is the most common way to talk about the future in English. It shows that a future action is planned, decided, or about to happen soon.

## When We Use "Be Going To"

- We use "be going to" for plans and intentions made before the moment of speaking. Example: I am going to visit my grandmother next weekend.

- We use it when we can see something will happen very soon, usually because we see evidence or signs now. Example: It is going to rain. Look at those dark clouds!

- We use it for decisions made before now, not decisions made at the moment of speaking. Example: We are going to move to a new house next month. This was decided yesterday.

- It expresses certainty or strong belief about something in the future. Example: You are going to love this film.

## How to Form "Be Going To"

- The structure is: subject + am/is/are + going to + base verb.

- Positive form: I am going to study English. He is going to call you tomorrow. They are going to play football.

- Negative form: Add "not" after the form of "be." I am not going to eat meat. She is not going to come to the party. We are not going to wait here.

- Question form: Put the form of "be" before the subject. Are you going to work today? Is he going to bring his friend? When are they going to arrive?

- The form of "be" must match the subject: I am, you are, he/she/it is, we are, they are.

## Spelling and Morphology Rules

- The base verb after "going to" never changes. You write "going to study," never "going to studies." The verb always keeps its base form.

- The form of "be" changes based on the subject. We must use the correct form: am with "I," is with he/she/it, and are with you/we/they.

- In contractions, we can shorten the form of "be": I'm going to, he's going to, they're going to. In negative contractions, we write "I'm not going to" or "isn't going to" but not "ain't."

- The word "going" is always the same. It never becomes "goes" or "gone" in this structure.

## Time Expressions with "Be Going To"

- Tomorrow, next week, next year, and next month are common time expressions. Example: I am going to see a doctor next week.

- "Soon," "in a few days," and "in two hours" tell us when something will happen. Example: The bus is going to arrive in ten minutes.

- "This weekend," "tonight," and "later" show when in the near future. Example: We are going to have dinner with friends this weekend.

- Without a time expression, the future action is often close to now. Example: I am going to sit down. This will happen very soon.

- We do not usually say "I am going to study" without telling when. It sounds incomplete. We add "tomorrow" or "next week" or another time marker.

## Common Mistakes Learners Make

- Learners often use the base verb form after "to" incorrectly. Wrong: I am going to goes. Correct: I am going to go.

- A mistake is using "will" instead of "be going to" for a plan made before now. Learners write "I will visit my mother tomorrow" when they should write "I am going to visit my mother tomorrow" because the decision was made earlier.

- Learners forget to use a form of "be." Wrong: I going to study. Correct: I am going to study.

- They sometimes add the wrong form of "be." Wrong: I am be going to study. Correct: I am going to study.

- Learners sometimes confuse the word order in questions. Wrong: You are going to what do? Correct: What are you going to do?

- A common mistake is using "be going to" for situations decided at the moment of speaking. Example: The phone is ringing. "I will answer it" is correct here, not "I am going to answer it." This decision happens right now, not before.
