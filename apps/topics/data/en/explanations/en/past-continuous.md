The past continuous, also called the past progressive, describes an action that was happening at a specific moment in the past or during a period in the past. It focuses on the action in progress, not its completion.

## When We Use the Past Continuous

- To describe an action that was in progress at a specific time in the past: I was studying at 9 PM last night.
- To describe a background action that was happening when another action happened: While I was cooking dinner, my friend arrived.
- To set the scene or provide context in storytelling: The sun was shining, the birds were singing, and everything seemed perfect.
- To describe two or more actions that were happening at the same time in the past: While she was reading, he was watching television.

## Forming the Positive

The past continuous uses the past tense of be (was or were) plus the present participle (the -ing form of the verb).

- Subject + was/were + verb-ing
- I was studying. / You were studying. / He was studying. / She was studying. / It was studying. / We were studying. / They were studying.
- The structure is always: was or were, followed immediately by the -ing form with no other words in between.

## Forming the Negative and Questions

To make the negative, add not after was or were. The contraction is wasn't or weren't.

- I was not studying. / I wasn't studying. / He wasn't studying. / They weren't studying.

To ask a question, move was or were to the beginning.

- Was I studying? / Were you studying? / Was he studying? / Were they studying?
- Short answers follow the pattern: Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. / Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.

## Rules for Adding -ing

When you add -ing to a verb, follow these spelling rules:

- Most verbs: add -ing directly: cook becomes cooking, run becomes running, study becomes studying.
- Verbs ending in silent e: drop the e before adding -ing: make becomes making, take becomes taking, live becomes living.
- One-syllable verbs with one vowel and one consonant: double the final consonant: stop becomes stopping, sit becomes sitting, plan becomes planning. But keep one consonant if the stress is not on the final syllable: listen becomes listening.
- Verbs ending in -ie: change ie to y before adding -ing: lie becomes lying, tie becomes tying, die becomes dying.

## Time Expressions and Markers

The past continuous often appears with specific time markers that show when or how long the action was happening.

- At + time: At 3 PM yesterday, I was sleeping.
- While + clause: While you were eating, I was cleaning.
- When + clause: When the earthquake happened, we were having dinner.
- All morning, afternoon, evening, or night: I was working all day.
- From... to: From 8 AM to 5 PM, she was in meetings.
- During: During the movie, the children were playing.

## Common Mistakes Learners Make

- Using simple past instead of past continuous: Incorrect: I studied when you called. Correct: I was studying when you called. The studying was in progress and the call interrupted it.
- Forgetting to use was or were: Incorrect: I studying at home. Correct: I was studying at home.
- Using was with plural subjects: Incorrect: They was running in the park. Correct: They were running in the park.
- Adding -ing to the wrong word: Incorrect: I was go to the store. Correct: I was going to the store.
- Confusing when and while: While shows two simultaneous actions (While I was cooking, he was reading). When shows an interruption (When I arrived, he was cooking).
- Using past continuous for a single completed action: Incorrect: I was went to the market yesterday. Correct: I went to the market yesterday. Use simple past when the action is complete.
