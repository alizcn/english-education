The third conditional is used to talk about imaginary situations in the past. It describes what would have happened if a different situation had existed. This form is essential for expressing regret, explaining why something did not happen, and discussing unrealistic past scenarios.

## When to use the third conditional

- Use the third conditional to describe a past event that did not happen and to imagine the result if it had happened
- It focuses on impossible situations because the past cannot be changed
- Common uses include expressing regret (If I had studied more, I would have passed the exam), explaining consequences (If it had rained, the game would have been cancelled), and imagining alternative histories (If she had taken that job, her life would have been different)
- The third conditional shows that the condition and result are both completely imaginary and contrary to what actually happened

## The form: positive sentences

- Third conditional uses if plus the past perfect in the condition clause: if + had + past participle
- The result clause uses would have plus the past participle: would have + past participle
- Structure: if + subject + had + past participle, subject + would have + past participle
- Examples: If you had told me earlier, I would have helped you. If they had left on time, they would have caught the train. If we had known about the party, we would have come.
- The condition can come first or second: If I had seen him, I would have said hello, or I would have said hello if I had seen him

## Negative sentences and questions

- To make a negative condition, use had not (or hadn't) in the if clause: if + subject + hadn't + past participle
- Example: If I hadn't been tired, I would have gone out
- To make a negative result, use would not have (or wouldn't have): subject + wouldn't have + past participle
- Example: If you had called me, I wouldn't have left early
- For questions, invert the would have part: Would you have gone if I had asked you? or If you had known, would you have changed your mind?
- To ask about the condition, use: What would have happened if he had arrived late?

## Time expressions and markers

- Third conditional typically occurs with past time expressions: yesterday, last week, last year, in 1990, at that time, back then
- Common phrases that signal third conditional thinking: If only, Had I known, Had they realized
- Certain connecting words introduce third conditional: If, In case, Provided that, As long as
- The word if can be replaced with had in formal written English: Had I known the truth, I would have acted differently (meaning the same as If I had known)
- Markers of regret or alternative thinking often accompany third conditional: unfortunately, sadly, luckily, fortunately, as it turned out

## Common mistakes learners make

- Using the wrong tenses: saying If I would have known instead of If I had known. The if clause must use had plus past participle, never would have
- Mixing conditionals: writing If I had known, I will help you (using will instead of would have)
- Incorrect past participles: saying If I had went instead of If I had gone, or If you had knew instead of If you had known
- Using simple past in the result clause: writing If you had told me, I help you instead of I would have helped you
- Forgetting have after would: saying would have called becomes would called
- Misplacing words in questions: saying Had you told me, would I have known? with incorrect word order
- Not recognizing that only the past perfect if clause creates third conditional meaning: third conditional requires if + had + past participle, or the sentence expresses a different meaning
