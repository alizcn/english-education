The zero conditional is the simplest conditional form in English. It expresses what always happens or what is always true when a particular condition is met. We use it for facts, scientific truths, and situations that are automatic or habitual.

## When We Use the Zero Conditional

- Use the zero conditional to express general truths about the world. If you heat water to 100 degrees Celsius, it boils. If you add salt to ice, the temperature drops.
- Use it for habitual or automatic actions. If my mother calls me, I always answer. If the alarm goes off, everyone wakes up.
- Use it for statements that are true all the time, not just in specific situations. If you practice every day, you improve. If you arrive late to class, the teacher marks you absent.
- The zero conditional describes cause and effect relationships that are predictable and constant.

## The Form and Structure

- The structure is always the same: if + present simple, then present simple. Both clauses use the present tense.
- The "if clause" comes first and introduces the condition. The "main clause" or "result clause" comes second and shows what happens.
- You can place the main clause first, followed by the if clause. The meaning stays the same: You improve if you practice every day.
- The word "then" is optional. Most speakers do not use it in everyday English: If you mix red and blue paint, you get purple or If you mix red and blue paint, then you get purple are both correct, but the first is more common.

## Positive Statements

- Positive zero conditionals follow the pattern: If + subject + present simple verb, subject + present simple verb.
- If you freeze water, it becomes ice.
- If the temperature drops below zero, the roads get dangerous.
- If a student studies hard, they pass the exam.
- If you boil an egg for ten minutes, the yolk becomes hard.
- In the if clause, use the base form of the verb with the correct subject. With "you", "I", "we", and "they", use the base form: If you touch the stove, you get burned. With "he", "she", or "it", add -s: If she eats too much sugar, she feels sick. If the sun shines, the snow melts.

## Negative Statements

- To make a negative zero conditional, add "not" after the auxiliary verb "do" or "does" in either clause or both.
- If you do not water the plants, they die.
- If he does not study, he fails the test.
- If she does not arrive on time, the meeting does not start.
- If you do not exercise, your health does not improve.
- Some learners use only "not" without the auxiliary: If you not water the plants is incorrect. You must use "do not" or "does not".
- You can make negative statements in only one clause: If it rains, people do not go outside. If you do not practice, your English does not improve.

## Questions

- To form zero conditional questions, put "do" or "does" at the beginning of the sentence, before the subject.
- Do you feel hungry if you skip breakfast? Yes, I do. No, I don't.
- Does water freeze if the temperature drops to zero degrees Celsius? Yes, it does.
- Does he get tired if he works all day? Yes, he does.
- What happens if you do not pay your electricity bill? What happens if you mix flour and water?
- In information questions, the question word comes at the beginning: What do you do if your car breaks down? Where do you go if the weather is bad? How do you feel if someone treats you badly?
- Remember to use "do" or "does", not just inversion. Do you get angry if someone is late? is correct. Get you angry if someone is late? is incorrect.

## Time Expressions and Markers

- Zero conditionals rarely use specific time expressions because they describe what always happens, not what will happen at a particular time.
- When, whenever, and if all introduce the condition in zero conditionals. When you add sugar to coffee, it tastes sweet. Whenever I call him, he answers. If you mix these colors, you get green.
- Some sentences use words like always, usually, or never to show that something happens automatically or regularly: If you eat too much, you always feel tired. If the alarm sounds, people usually wake up. If you do not study, you never understand the material.
- Adverbs of frequency often appear in zero conditionals to emphasize the automatic nature: If children spend too much time on screens, they often develop bad posture. If you use the same password everywhere, you usually have problems.

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Confusing zero conditional with first conditional. Zero conditional = always true. First conditional = possible future event. Incorrect: If you will eat too much, you feel sick. Correct: If you eat too much, you feel sick. You never use "will" in a zero conditional.
- Using future tense in the if clause. Incorrect: If you will exercise tomorrow, you will feel better. Correct: If you exercise, you feel better.
- Forgetting "do" or "does" in negative statements. Incorrect: If you not study, you fail. Correct: If you do not study, you fail.
- Adding "then" with the wrong meaning. While "then" is grammatically acceptable, learners sometimes think it changes the meaning. It does not. If you heat water, it boils and If you heat water, then it boils mean the same thing.
- Using the wrong auxiliary verb form. Incorrect: Does the ice melts if the sun shines? Correct: Does the ice melt if the sun shines? Use the base form after the auxiliary.
- Misplacing the if clause. Many learners believe the if clause must come first, but both orders are correct: If you practice, you improve and You improve if you practice are equally correct.
- Forgetting that both clauses must use present simple. Incorrect: If you heat water, it will boil. Correct: If you heat water, it boils. Zero conditional never uses will or other future forms.
