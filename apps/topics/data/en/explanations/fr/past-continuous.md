Le Past Continuous décrit une action qui était en cours à un moment spécifique du passé. C'est le temps de l'action interrompue, de l'atmosphère, ou de deux événements qui se déroulaient simultanément. Il combine l'auxiliaire être au passé (was ou were) avec la forme -ing du verbe.

## Quand utiliser le Past Continuous

- Pour décrire une action en cours à un moment donné du passé: I was reading when you called.
- Pour exprimer une interruption: She was sleeping when the earthquake happened.
- Pour montrer deux actions simultanées: While he was cooking, she was setting the table.
- Pour créer une atmosphère ou un contexte: It was raining, and the wind was blowing hard.
- Pour une action non terminée dans le passé: They were living in Paris in 2020 (but they moved away).

## Construction du Past Continuous

La structure est simple: was ou were + verbe principal + -ing.

- Was s'utilise avec les sujets singuliers (I, he, she, it).
- Were s'utilise avec les sujets pluriels (you, we, they).
- Exemples: I was walking. He was sleeping. They were playing.

Remarquez que was est irrégulier au passé; on ne dit jamais "I am-ed" ou "I were" au singulier.

## Formes affirmative, négative et interrogative

- Affirmative: They were watching the film. / The dog was barking loudly.
- Négative: You were not listening. / She was not paying attention. (On peut aussi écrire wasn't et weren't.)
- Interrogative: Were they studying? / Was he sleeping? (L'auxiliaire passe avant le sujet.)
- Réponse courte: Yes, I was. / No, they were not.

## Règles de morphologie pour la terminaison -ing

- La plupart des verbes ajoutent simplement -ing: walk → walking, play → playing.
- Si le verbe se termine par un e muet, on l'enlève: make → making, drive → driving, write → writing.
- Si le verbe est court et se termine par une consonne précédée d'une voyelle, on double la consonne finale: run → running, swim → swimming, stop → stopping. (Mais pas si le verbe se termine par deux consonnes: help → helping.)
- Les verbes en -ie changent en -ying: die → dying, lie → lying.

## Expressions temporelles et marqueurs

- While (pendant que): While I was cooking, the phone rang.
- When (quand, à ce moment-là): When they arrived, we were eating dinner.
- As (au moment où): As she was leaving, she saw an old friend.
- At that moment / at that time: At that moment, he was thinking about his future.
- Yesterday at 5 p.m. / on Monday afternoon: I was working at 3 p.m. yesterday.
- All day / all night: She was working all day.

## Les erreurs courantes des apprenants

- Confondre Past Continuous et Past Simple. On dit "I was eating when he arrived" (une action continue interrompue), pas "I ate when he arrived" (deux actions isolées).
- Oublier le verbe être: On dit "They were playing", pas "They playing".
- Ajouter deux fois -ing. On dit "I was writing", pas "I was writting" ou "I was writting".
- Utiliser went au lieu de was going: "I was going to the store" (intention au passé), pas "I went to the store" (sauf si vous avez réellement terminé le voyage).
- Mélanger was et were: "He was" (correct), pas "He were".
- Oublier d'enlever le e muet: "I was making" (correct), pas "I was makeing".

## Past Continuous versus Past Simple

- Past Continuous met l'accent sur la durée ou le processus: He was reading for two hours.
- Past Simple marque une action terminée ou isolée: He read the book in two hours.
- Combinés dans une phrase narrative: He was reading when I called. (Il lisait déjà; j'ai interrompu.)
- Les deux dans une suite chronologique: First, they were talking. Then, they left. (Mais cette formulation est rare; on dirait plutôt "They talked and then left.")
