These three verbal adverb suffixes mark the relationship between two actions or events in a sentence. Each suffix gives different information about timing and manner. Understanding which to use depends on whether actions happen at the same time, in sequence, or how one action leads to another.

## The -ken Suffix

The -ken suffix shows that two actions happen at the same time. It answers the question "while" or "when" in the sense of "during the time that." The action marked with -ken is ongoing when the main action occurs.

- Kitap okurken müzik dinlerim: I listen to music while reading a book.
- Ders dinlerken telefon kullandı: He used his phone while listening to the lesson.
- Çay içerken gazetesini okudu: While drinking tea, he read his newspaper.
- Arabayı sürerken şarkı söylüyordu: While driving the car, he was singing.

The -ken suffix attaches to the verb stem. When the verb uses the -yor tense, the form is geliyorken (while coming), bekliyorken (while waiting). With past tense, you may see gelmişken or geldiği halde for "although he had come," though these are less common than -ken with the present or ongoing stems.

## The -ınca Suffix

The -ınca suffix (spelled -ince, -unca, or -üncü based on vowel harmony) shows that one action happens after another or as a result of the first. It answers "when" in the sense of "as soon as" or "once." The action marked with -ınca happens first; the main action happens afterward.

- Kapıyı açınca ben hemen içeri girdim: Once he opened the door, I immediately went inside.
- Kahve içince uyanıyor: Once he drinks coffee, he wakes up.
- Sorunun cevabını bulunca bana telefon etti: When he found the answer to the question, he called me.
- Saat sekize gelince iş bitmiş olacak: When it reaches eight o'clock, the work will be finished.

The -ınca suffix follows regular vowel harmony rules. After a or ı, it becomes -ınca; after e or i, it becomes -ince; after o or u, it becomes -unca; after ö or ü, it becomes -üncü. These forms attach directly to the verb stem: açmak becomes açınca, gelmek becomes gelince.

## The -arak/-erek Suffix

The -arak or -erek suffix (depending on vowel harmony) shows manner, means, or how something is done. It can also show sequential actions where one action leads naturally to another. It answers "by doing" or "how."

- Hızlı koşarak eve vardı: He arrived home by running fast.
- Oyun oynayarak öğreniyoruz: We learn by playing.
- Kapıyı açarak içeri girdi: Opening the door, he went inside (sequential action).
- Yürüyerek şehri geride bıraktı: By walking, he left the city behind.

Vowel harmony applies: after back vowels (a, ı, o, u), use -arak; after front vowels (e, i, ö, ü), use -erek. The suffix attaches directly to the verb stem: koşmak becomes koşarak, gelmek becomes gelerek. This suffix is flexible; it can express manner (how the action is done), means (what method is used), or show two actions happening in sequence where the first naturally leads to the second.

## Negative Forms and Complications

Negative forms with these suffixes are uncommon or unnatural in Turkish, and learners should be aware of better alternatives.

- With -ken: gelmezken exists technically (while not coming) but is rare. Better alternatives are gelmediği halde (although not coming) or gelmediğine rağmen (despite not coming). Avoid forcing negative -ken forms.

- With -ınca: Turkish does not use a standard negative -ınca form. Instead, speakers say gelmediği zaman (when he did not come) or gelmediğinde (when he did not come). The construction açmadıkça (as long as he does not open) offers a negative meaning but uses a different suffix altogether.

- With -arak: yazmazarak (by not writing) or yazmazdan (without writing) exists but sounds awkward. More natural is to restructure: yazıp gitmez (does not write and go), or use yazması (his writing), or simply say yazma (not writing).

When you need negation with these temporal relationships, restructure your sentence rather than force a negative suffix form.

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Confusing -ken with -ınca: Both can translate to "when," but their meanings differ. Yemek yerken means "while eating," showing actions at the same time. Yemek yiyince means "after eating" or "once eating is done," showing that one action follows another. Choose based on whether the actions are simultaneous or sequential.

- Forgetting vowel harmony: The -ınca and -arak/-erek suffixes must follow vowel harmony. For example, gelmek requires gelince (not gelinca), and alkışlamak requires alkışlayarak (not alkışlarak). Learners often apply the wrong vowel to the suffix.

- Assuming -arak only means manner: While -arak often expresses how or by what means something is done, it can also show sequential action. Kapıyı açarak gitti means "opening the door, he left" or "he left by opening the door," showing an action that naturally leads into another.

- Using -yor with -ken incorrectly: Geliyorken is correct (while he is coming), but some learners might say geliyip-ken or gelmiş-ken in the wrong contexts. The -yor stem plus -ken is the most natural pairing for ongoing simultaneous actions.

- Mixing up stem forms: Some learners attach -ınca or -arak to the past stem (geldi-nce) or other conjugated forms instead of the verb root (gelmek stem: gel). Remember that these suffixes attach to the bare verb stem, not to inflected forms.
