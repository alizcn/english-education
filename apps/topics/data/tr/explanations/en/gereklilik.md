The Turkish necessity mood (gereklilik kipi) expresses obligation, necessity, and what must be done. It uses the suffixes -malı or -meli depending on vowel harmony. This form is essential for expressing duties, recommendations, and requirements in Turkish.

## When the Necessity Mood is Used

- To express obligation or requirement: Yarın erkenden başlamalıyız (We must start early tomorrow)
- To indicate what is necessary or must happen: Bu işi yapmalısın (You must do this work)
- To express advice or strong recommendation: Daha az kahve içmelisin (You should drink less coffee)
- To show duty or responsibility: Evini temizlemeli misin? (Do you have to clean your house?)
- To express natural or logical necessity: Çok sıcaksa, hemen su içmelisin (If it is very hot, you must drink water immediately)

## Building the Necessity Mood: Positive Form

The basic structure is: verb stem + -malı/-meli + personal suffix

- For vowel harmony: use -malı after words with back vowels (a, ı, o, u) and -meli after words with front vowels (e, i, ö, ü)
- The last vowel of the stem determines which suffix to use
- Examples with back vowels: Ben yapmalıyım (I must do), Sen konuşmalısın (You must speak)
- Examples with front vowels: Ben gelmeliyim (I must come), Sen ümit etmelisin (You must hope)
- The personal suffix follows standard Turkish conjugation: third person singular has no suffix, and other persons add -yım/-ım/-um/-üm, -sın/-sin/-sun/-sün, -yız/-ız/-uz/-üz, -sınız/-siniz/-sunuz/-sünüz, or -lar/-ler

## Building the Necessity Mood: Negative and Question Forms

- Negative: attach -me/-ma to the stem (following vowel harmony), then add -malı/-meli: yapmalı becomes yapmamalı (must not do)
- Examples: Buraya gelmemeliyiz (We must not come here), Bu konuyu konuşmamalısın (You must not talk about this topic)
- Question: place -mı/-mi/-mu/-mü after the personal suffix: yapmalı mıyım? (Must I do?), gelmelisin mi? (Must you come?)
- Combined negative question: yapmamalı mıyım? (Must I not do?), çalışmamalı mısın? (Must you not work?)

## Spelling and Morphology Rules

- Always determine vowel harmony by the last vowel in the verb stem
- For third person singular, no personal suffix is added: Gelmeli (He/she/it must come)
- For other persons, the personal suffix attaches directly; when the suffix begins with a vowel, a -y- appears between the stem and suffix: gelmeliyim, gelmelisin
- In negative forms, -me/-ma is inserted between stem and -malı/-meli: git becomes gitmemeliyim (I must not go)
- Irregular verbs follow the same pattern: etmek (to do) becomes etmeliyim (I must do)
- When a stem ends in a consonant cluster that would be difficult to pronounce with the suffix, the natural pronunciation determines the form

## Time Expressions and Markers

- The necessity mood inherently expresses future or current obligation: Yarın saat 8'de başlamalısın (You must start at 8 o'clock tomorrow)
- Common time markers: yarın (tomorrow), sonra (later), hemen (immediately), bugün (today), her zaman (always), şimdi (now)
- The form can express present obligation: Şimdi gitmelisin (You must go now)
- Present tense interpretations are common: Ödevini yapmalısın (You must do your homework) often refers to regular or immediate tasks
- The necessity mood does not have separate past forms in modern conversational Turkish; context indicates time reference

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Confusing stem vowel harmony with infinitive vowels: the infinitive gelmek has different vowels than the stem gel, which determines the -meli choice
- Reversing vowel harmony: using -malı after a front-vowel stem like gelmalı instead of gelmelidir
- Omitting -me/-ma in negative forms: saying gelmesiz instead of gelmemeliyiz (we must not come)
- Wrong personal suffix: gelmeli alone is only for third person; I must come requires gelmeliyim, not just gelmeli
- Mixing necessity mood with conditional: gelmeli (must come) differs from gelsem (if I come) or gelmiş olsam (if I had come)
- Forgetting question marker placement: gelmelisiniz mi? is correct, not gelmeli misiniz?
- Using necessity mood when a different expression is appropriate: yapmalısın (you must do) is too forceful for optional actions; use yapabilirsin (you can do) or yapsan iyi olur (it would be good if you did) instead
