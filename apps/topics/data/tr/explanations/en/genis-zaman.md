Geniş Zaman is the simple present or habitual past tense in Turkish, used to express actions that happen regularly, statements of general fact, or past habitual actions. It is marked by the suffix -r, -ar, or -ir (chosen by vowel harmony), and is one of the first tenses you will encounter when learning Turkish. Understanding its forms and proper use is essential for everyday conversation.

## When Geniş Zaman is Used

- For repeated or habitual actions in the present: Her gün yürüyüş yaparım. (I walk every day.)
- For general truths or facts: Kedi bağımsız bir hayvandır. (A cat is an independent animal.)
- For habitual or characteristic actions in the past: Çocukken oyun oynardık. (When we were children, we used to play.)
- For actions that are not tied to a specific moment: Kahveyi severim. (I like coffee.)

## How to Form Geniş Zaman: Positive

- Take the verb stem and add the person suffix (-ım, -ız, -sin, etc.) with the appropriate tense marker (-r, -ar, or -ir) inserted before it.
- The choice of -r, -ar, or -ir depends on vowel harmony with the final vowel of the stem.
- After stems ending in a or ı: add -ar: Çalışarım. (I work.)
- After stems ending in e or i: add -ir: Gelirsin. (You come.)
- After stems ending in o or u: add -or: Oturorum. (I sit.)
- After stems ending in ö or ü: add -ür: Öğrenürüz. (We learn.)

## Negative Form

- Add -maz or -mez (following vowel harmony) between the stem and the person suffix: Çalışmam. (I do not work.)
- The negative marker -m- is inserted, then the tense marker, then the person suffix: gel + mez + sin = Gelmezsin. (You do not come.)
- Common stems follow this pattern: Okumazsınız. (You do not read.) Yardım etmez. (He does not help.)

## Question Form

- Add the question particle -mi (-mı, -mu, -mü) after the tense and person suffix: Çalışar mısın? (Do you work?)
- The word order is flexible, but the question particle typically comes at the end of the verb phrase: Siz kahve içer misiniz? (Do you drink coffee?)
- In negative questions, -maz or -mez comes first, then the question particle: Gelmez misin? (Don't you come?)

## Vowel Harmony and Spelling Rules

- Vowel harmony is crucial: the tense marker must match the vowel of the stem.
- Front vowels (e, i, ö, ü) pair with -ir and -ür; back vowels (a, ı, o, u) pair with -ar and -or.
- After soft consonants (c, ç, g, ğ, j, s, ş, z, y), the marker is softer: kör + ü + r = körür (one is blind / happens to be blind), not körur.
- One-syllable verbs often take just -r: gelmez, gelirsin, versus buyurur (from buyur).

## Common Time Expressions and Markers

- Geniş Zaman often appears with adverbs of frequency or habit: Her gün, her hafta, her ay, her yıl (every day, week, month, year).
- Temporal markers like çoğunlukla (usually), bazen (sometimes), sık sık (often), asla (never) frequently accompany this tense: Bazen sinemaya giderim. (I sometimes go to the cinema.)
- For past habitual actions, expressions like "çocukken" (when I was a child), "o zamanlar" (back then), or "vaktiyle" (in the past) signal habitual past: Vaktiyle her sabah koşardım. (In the past, I used to run every morning.)

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Mixing up -ar and -ir forms: Writing "Gelmez" when you mean "gelir" (comes), or "okurum" when it should be "okurum." Double-check vowel harmony with the stem.
- Forgetting the tense marker entirely: Saying "gelim" instead of "gelirim" (I come), or "gelsem" which has a different meaning (if I come, were I to come).
- Incorrectly applying -mi in questions: The particle attaches to the final word of the verb phrase, not the stem. "Gelmimi?" is wrong; "Gelir misin?" is correct.
- Confusing Geniş Zaman with Geçmiş Zaman or Şimdiki Zaman: Remember that this tense emphasizes habit or fact, not a one-time action or what is happening right now. "Okurum" is what I (habitually) do; "okudum" is what I did (once); "okuyorum" is what I am doing (right now).
- Over-extending it to specific moments in the past: You cannot say "Dün saat 3'te gelmedi, bugün gelir." (Yesterday at 3 o'clock he didn't come, today he comes.) Use Past Tense for specific past moments, not Geniş Zaman.
