Var and yok are the fundamental existential verbs in Turkish, expressing existence and possession. Var means "there is," "exists," or "has," while yok means "there is not," "doesn't exist," or "doesn't have." Unlike English, Turkish uses the same structures to express both existence and possession.

## What "var" and "yok" mean

Var means existence, presence, or possession. Yok means non-existence, absence, or lack. These words function as verbs, though they behave differently from action verbs. Unlike English, which uses different words for existence ("is there") and possession ("has"), Turkish uses var for both.

- Bir kedi var. = There is a cat.
- Paraları var. = They have money.
- Su yok. = There is no water.
- Benim bir arabam var. = I have a car.

## How to form positive statements

To express existence or possession with var, place var after the object or possessed item. The word var itself doesn't change form, but what comes before it depends on what you're talking about. If expressing possession, the possessor typically comes in the nominative case, and the possessed item shows possession with a suffix.

- Evde bir kitap var. = There is a book in the house.
- Ali'nin bir kedisi var. = Ali has a cat.
- Benim bir bilgisayarım var. = I have a computer.
- Su var. = There is water.

## How to form negative statements

To negate, replace var with yok. Everything else remains the same. Yok also doesn't conjugate. The construction is identical to var, just with yok instead.

- Evde bir kitap yok. = There is no book in the house.
- Ali'nin bir kedisi yok. = Ali doesn't have a cat.
- Benim para yok. = I don't have money.
- Su yok. = There is no water.

## How to form questions

To ask a question with var/yok, add the question particle mi after var or yok. Mi becomes mı, mi, mu, or mu based on vowel harmony. You can also use rising intonation, but mi makes it explicitly a yes/no question.

- Paraları var mı? = Do they have money?
- Evde birisi var mı? = Is there someone in the house?
- Senin bir arabı var mı? = Do you have a car?
- Su yok mu? = Is there no water?

## Possession with pronouns

With possessive pronouns, Turkish uses a consistent pattern. The pronoun in nominative case comes first, followed by the possessed item with a possessive suffix, then var or yok.

- Benim bir kedim var. = I have a cat.
- Senin bir kitabı var. = You have a book.
- Onun bir arabası var. = He/she has a car.
- Bizim bir evimiz var. = We have a house.
- Sizin bir probleminiz var. = You (plural/formal) have a problem.

## Common learner mistakes

Many learners add conjugation endings to var or yok, but they never change form. Learners often forget the possessive suffix on the owned item. Some confuse yok with negative verb forms like değil; yok is specifically for non-existence or non-possession.

- Incorrect: Bir arabam varm. = Correct: Bir arabam var.
- Incorrect: Benim para var. = Correct: Benim param var.
- Incorrect: Bir kitabı yok var. = Correct: Bir kitabı yok.
- Incorrect: Ben var mi? = Correct: Var mıyım? (for "Do I exist?")

## Time expressions and past forms

Var and yok don't conjugate for tense. In modern Turkish, time adverbs accompany the present-tense forms. For past existence, speakers use vardı or yoktu, which express "there was" or "there was not." Future existence uses olacak with yok: yok olacak means "there won't be."

- Dün bir kedi vardı. = There was a cat yesterday.
- Yarın hiçbir para yok. = Tomorrow there will be no money.
- Geçen sene bir arabası vardı. = He had a car last year.
- Burada bir kütüphane var. = There is a library here.
