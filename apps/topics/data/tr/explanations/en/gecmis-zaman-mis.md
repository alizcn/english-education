The Duyulan Geçmiş Zaman, often called the evidential past or reported past, expresses actions or events that the speaker did not witness directly. The speaker reports information they heard, read, or inferred from evidence. This tense is one of the most characteristic features of Turkish and shows the speaker's attitude toward the information.

## When to Use the -mış Form

- Use -mış when you did not see something happen yourself, but heard about it from someone else: Dün bir kaza olmuş. (There was an accident yesterday.)
- Use it to express surprise or sudden realization about something: Oğlan doktor olmuş! (The boy has become a doctor!)
- Use it for information you learned indirectly or after the fact: Toplantı saat üçte başlamış. (The meeting started at three.)
- Use it when you infer something from visual evidence: Yağmur yağmış. (It rained.)
- Use it in storytelling when recounting what others told you: Kral bir gece düşünde biraz para harcamış. (In his dream one night, the king spent some money.)

## Building the Positive Form

The positive form uses the suffix -mış attached to the verb stem, followed by personal endings. The suffix follows vowel harmony: back vowel stems take -mış, front vowel stems take -miş.

- Back vowel stems (a, ı, o, u): add -mış: kalmak becomes kalmış, almak becomes almış, olmak becomes olmuş
- Front vowel stems (e, i, ö, ü): add -miş: gelmek becomes gelmiş, vermek becomes vermiş, gülmek becomes gülmüş

Personal conjugation:

- ben gelmişim (I came)
- sen gelmişsin (you came)
- o gelmiş (he/she came)
- biz gelmişiz (we came)
- siz gelmişsiniz (you all came)
- onlar gelmişler (they came)

More examples: Kitabı okumuş. (He read the book.) Kızlar gülmüşler. (The girls laughed.) Çay demlemişim. (I apparently made tea.)

## Building the Negative Form

To form the negative, place the negative suffix -me or -ma between the verb stem and -mış, following vowel harmony. Then add personal endings as normal.

- Back vowel stems take -mamış: kalmamış, almamış, olmamış
- Front vowel stems take -memişş: gelmemiş, vermemiş, gülmemiş

Personal conjugation:

- ben gelmemişim (I apparently did not come)
- sen gelmemişsin (you apparently did not come)
- o gelmemiş (he/she apparently did not come)
- biz gelmemişiz (we apparently did not come)
- siz gelmemişsiniz (you all apparently did not come)
- onlar gelmemişler (they apparently did not come)

Other examples: Okumamış. (He apparently did not read.) Gülmemişler. (They apparently did not laugh.) Evini satmamış. (He apparently did not sell his house.)

## Building Questions

To form a question, add the question particle -mi, -mı, -mu, or -mü after the personal suffix. The particle follows the four-way vowel harmony of Turkish.

- Gelmiş misin? (Did you apparently come?)
- Gelmiş mi? (Did he apparently come?)
- Gitmişler mi? (Did they apparently go?)
- Okumuş musunuz? (Did you apparently read?)

You can also use rising intonation alone: Gelmiş? or Gitmiş? without adding the question particle.

## Vowel Harmony and Consonant Adjustments

The -mış suffix strictly follows Turkish vowel harmony, which depends on the last vowel of the verb stem.

- If the stem ends with a or ı (back, unrounded): add -mış: kalmış, salmış, açmış
- If the stem ends with o or u (back, rounded): add -mış: olmuş, bulmuş, yorulmuş
- If the stem ends with e or i (front, unrounded): add -miş: gelmiş, vermiş, dinlemiş
- If the stem ends with ö or ü (front, rounded): add -miş: dönemiş, gülmüş, sürülmüş

Consonant softening: Voiceless consonants (p, ç, t, k) at the end of a stem do not double before the vowel suffix. Write çalmış, not çalpmış; tutmuş, not tuttmuş; katmış, not kattmış.

## Common Time Expressions with -mış

While the -mış form does not require a time expression, certain adverbials frequently accompany it to clarify when the reported event occurred.

- Dün (yesterday): Dün yağmur yağmış. (It rained yesterday.)
- Geçen hafta (last week): Geçen hafta onu görmüşüm. (I saw him last week.)
- Geçen ay (last month): Geçen ay yeni bir iş bulmuş. (He found a new job last month.)
- Geçen sene (last year): Geçen sene evlenmişler. (They got married last year.)
- O zaman (at that time): O zaman hiç kimse gelmemiş. (At that time, no one came.)
- Uzun zaman önce (a long time ago): Uzun zaman önce bir kral yaşamış. (A king lived a long time ago.)
- Bir gün (one day): Bir gün kapı çalmış. (One day someone knocked on the door.)

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Confusing -mış with the simple past -di: The -di form indicates you witnessed the action (Dün sinemaya gittim = I went to the cinema yesterday, and I was there). The -mış form indicates reported or inferred information (Dün sinemaya gitmiş = He went to the cinema yesterday, according to what you heard). Use -di for your own experiences, -mış for reported events.
- Using -mış for your own actions in formal contexts: Do not use -mış for simple narratives about yourself. Your own experience uses -di: Dükkana yürüdüm. (I walked to the store.) Use -mış only when you are reporting what happened to you with surprise or reporting what you heard.
- Forgetting personal endings in first and second person: Ben gelmiş is incomplete. Always use ben gelmişim, sen gelmişsin. These personal endings are necessary.
- Misplacing the negative suffix: The negative -me/-ma must come between the stem and -mış: Gelmemişim is correct; gelmişmem is wrong.
- Incorrectly doubling final consonants: Write okumuş, not okutmuş. Write gülmüş, not gülmmüş. The final consonant does not double.
- Using -mış for established historical facts in formal writing: Atatürk 1881 yılında doğdu. (Atatürk was born in 1881 - correct for documented fact). Using -mış in formal history writing sounds like legend or indirect report, which can be inappropriate.
