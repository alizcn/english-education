The imperative mood (emir kipi) and optative mood (istek kipi) are two related moods in Turkish that express commands, requests, and wishes. The imperative gives direct orders, while the optative expresses hopes, desires, or softer suggestions. Both moods follow similar patterns but create very different tones and social contexts.

## When to Use the Imperative Mood

- Use the imperative to give direct commands: Git! (Go!)
- Use it for instructions or urgent requests: Pencereyi aç. (Open the window.)
- Use it with people of equal or lower status; it can sound rude with elders or strangers
- The imperative assumes the listener will obey or comply
- Common in everyday speech with friends and family: Gel buraya! (Come here!)

## When to Use the Optative Mood

- The optative expresses a wish or hope about what might happen: Kazanasın. (I hope you win.)
- Use it to express a desired condition: Eğer yapabilseydim... (If only I could do it...)
- Use it for polite suggestions that respect the listener's choice: Bir kahve içseydik? (How about we have coffee?)
- It sounds softer and more respectful than direct commands
- Often appears in blessings and well-wishes: Sağlıcakla kalasın. (Stay healthy and well.)

## Building the Imperative Mood

- For sen (informal you), use the bare verb root: Oku! (Read!), Yaz! (Write!)
- For siz (formal you), add -ın, -in, -un, or -ün: Okuyun! Yazın! (Read! Write!)
- For o (he/she/it), use the root plus -sın, -sin, -sun, or -sün: Okuysun! Yazısın! (Let him read! Let her write!)
- For biz (we), add -alım or -elim: Okuyalım! Yazalım! (Let us read! Let us write!)
- Vowel harmony always applies: gel- becomes geliniz (go, formal), oku- becomes okuyun (read, formal)

## Building the Optative Mood

- The optative uses the conditional marker -se or -sa (vowel harmony)
- For ben (I), use -sem or -sam: Okusam. (If I were to read, I wish I could read.)
- For sen (you), use -sen or -san: Okusaysan. (If only you would read.)
- For o (he/she/it), use -se or -sa: Okusaysa. (If only he/she would read.)
- For biz (we), use -sek or -sak: Okuseydik. (If only we could read.)
- Past optative adds -ydı, -ydu, -idi, or -udi after the -se form: Gelmeseydi (If only he hadn't come)
- The optative frequently appears with yardımcı fiiller (auxiliary verbs): Bilsem (If I only knew / I wish I knew)

## Negative Forms in Both Moods

- For imperative negation, add -ma or -me between verb and mood marker: Gitme! (Don't go!), Yazma! (Don't write!)
- Formal negative imperative: Gitmeyin! Yazmayin! (Don't go/write! - to siz)
- For optative negation, place -ma/-me before the -se/-sa: Gitmesem. (If I didn't go / I wish I hadn't gone.)
- Past negative optative: Gitmeseydi. (If only he hadn't gone.)
- Common with "hiç" for emphasis: Hiç gitmesem! (I really hope I don't go!)

## Question Formation

- In imperative, add -mi/-mı/-mu/-mü or use intonation: Gitsin mi? (Should he go?)
- This transforms a command into a polite request for permission: Bir kahve içeyim mi? (May I have some coffee?)
- In optative, the question marker comes at the end: Okusam mı? (Should I read?)
- Sometimes the question particle is optional, and rising intonation alone signals a question
- These forms are much more polite than direct imperatives

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Confusing optative with conditional: "Okuseydim" (optative: if only I had read) is not the same as "Okursam" (conditional: if I read)
- Forgetting vowel harmony in formal imperatives: Saying "Gitmeyin" instead of "Gitmeyin" is correct, but "Gitmayin" violates harmony
- Using the bare sen imperative with elders or strangers; always use siz form for politeness
- Placing the negative marker incorrectly: "Magilme" is wrong; it must be "Gitme"
- Adding tense markers to imperatives: "Okuyacaksın" is future tense, not a command
- Confusing "Okusam" (optative: if I could read) with "Okudum" (past simple: I read)
- Using imperatives that sound too harsh in formal settings; the optative with -seydik is softer
- Misplacing the auxiliary verb in optative forms: "Bilsem" (if I could know) not "Bilem"

## Morphology and Vowel Harmony Rules

- Vowel harmony is mandatory in both moods: oku- (back vowel) takes -un, not -en
- Verbs ending in vowels require -y- insertion before mood markers: sev- becomes seveyim (I want), seveyin (read/pl), sevmeyin (don't/pl)
- The optative -se/-sa is historically related to the conditional; both use the same marker
- Compare: "Okurum" (I read - present) and "Okuyorum" (I am reading - present continuous); imperatives ignore tense entirely
- After auxiliary verbs like "bil" (can), the optative becomes "bilsem" (if I could, I wish I could)
- Time markers rarely appear with imperatives: "Yarın git!" (Go tomorrow!) sounds awkward; use future instead
- The optative with future meaning uses the -se form without the past -ydi suffix: "Gelsem" can mean "What if I were to come?"
