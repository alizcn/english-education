The Turkish ability mood, marked by the suffix -abilmek or -ebilmek, expresses whether someone can or is able to do something. It is one of the core ways Turkish speakers describe capability, possibility, or permission. You will encounter this form constantly in conversation and writing.

## When and Why You Use the Ability Mood

The ability mood describes what is possible or within someone's power to do. It appears in sentences about skill, permission, circumstance, and potential. You use it when you want to say that an action can happen, could happen, or is possible. Time does not change the core function; whether you speak about the past, present, or future, the ability mood marks feasibility.

- Ben türkçe konuşabilirim. (I can speak Turkish.)
- Bugün gelmek istiyorum ama gelemeyeceğim. (I want to come today but I won't be able to come.)
- Bunu yapabilir misin? (Can you do this?)
- Herkes buraya girebilir. (Anyone can enter here.)

## Building the Positive Form

Start with the verb stem. Remove -mek or -mak from the infinitive to get the stem. Add -abilir or -ebilir depending on vowel harmony. The form you add carries tense and person markers after it. The first -a- or -e- is part of the ability suffix; what follows shows tense (present, past, future) and subject (I, you, he/she, etc.).

- gelmek → gel + abilir → gelabilirim (I can come)
- almak → al + abilir → alabilirim (I can take)
- görmek → gör + ebilir → görebilirim (I can see)
- duşmek → düş + ebilir → düşebilirim (I can fall)

Notice that after certain consonants, the form may change slightly. After vowel-final stems, you insert a buffer: gelişabilirim (I can improve). After stems ending in -l, -r, -ş, or -z, the -e- form is common. The suffix agrees with the vowel harmony of the verb stem.

## Building the Negative Form

To make the ability mood negative, add -eme or -ama between the stem and the ability suffix. This produces a double suffix pattern: stem + -eme/ama + -bile + tense and person. Many learners mistakenly place negation after the ability suffix; this is wrong. The negation sits before the ability marker.

- gelmek → gel + eme + bilir → gelemem (I cannot come)
- almak → al + ama + bilir → alamamız (we cannot take)
- görmek → gör + eme + bilir → göremez (he/she cannot see)
- duşmek → düş + eme + bilir → düşemez (he/she cannot fall)

Watch out: the form gelemiyorum means "I am not coming" (a simple present negative), but gelemem means "I cannot come" (inability). These are different.

## Building the Question Form

To ask a question using the ability mood, add the question particle mi/mı/mu/mü to the end of the full ability mood form. The particle agrees with the vowel of the final syllable. You can also rearrange word order or use intonation, but the particle is the clearest signal of a question.

- Gelabilir misin? (Can you come?)
- Türkçe konuşabiliyor musun? (Can you speak Turkish?)
- Burayı görebilir miyiz? (Can we see this place?)
- Bunu yapamayacak mı? (Won't he/she be unable to do this?)

The negative question combines the negative ability form with the question particle: gelemez misin? (Can't you come?). The position of the particle never changes; it always comes at the very end.

## Vowel Harmony and Spelling Rules

Turkish vowel harmony is strict with the ability suffix. The stem's vowels determine whether you use -abilir or -ebilir. Back vowels (a, ı, o, u) in the stem → -abilir. Front vowels (e, i, ö, ü) in the stem → -ebilir. If the stem ends in a vowel and is followed by the ability suffix, a buffer consonant (usually -y-) may appear: kalabilirim becomes kalabiliyorum when you add a further tense or person suffix.

- Stems with back vowels: yapabilir (can do), kalabilir (can stay), korkabilir (can be afraid), okuyabilir (can read)
- Stems with front vowels: görebilir (can see), gelişebilir (can improve), yüzebilir (can swim)

The consonant at the end of the stem also matters. After -ll, -rr, -şş, or -zz, or after single consonants like -p, -ç, -k, -t, some stems take -ebilir by convention. These rules overlap and can seem chaotic at first; exposure to many verbs will train your intuition.

## Common Learner Mistakes

Learners often place negation in the wrong spot. You might hear gelemiyorum from a learner who meant "I cannot come," but this actually means "I am not coming right now." The correct form is gelemem (present negation of ability) or gelemeyeceğim (future negation).

Another error is forgetting vowel harmony. If you say gelabilirim with a front-vowel stem like "görmek," this sounds very unnatural. Always match the vowel in -abilir or -ebilir to the stem's vowel color.

A third mistake is using the ability suffix when you should use a simple present or future tense. Turkish speakers do not say konuşabilirim when they mean "I speak Turkish" as a general fact; they say konuşurum. Reserve the ability mood for describing whether something is possible or within someone's capability in a given context.

Finally, some learners confuse the ability mood with the potential mood expressed through -abilir plus a past tense: yapabilmişim (I was able to do). This is a real form, but it has a different nuance—often suggesting surprise or unexpectedness. In everyday speech, use the simple ability form most of the time.
