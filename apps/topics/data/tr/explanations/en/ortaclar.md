These participles (ortaçlar) are verb forms that function as adjectives or in relative clauses, showing time relationships. They are built from the verb root using vowel harmony rules and are among the most frequently used structures in Turkish.

## The -an/-en Participle: General and Present Actions

The -an/-en form describes habitual, general, or present-moment actions. It is the most common participle in Turkish.

- Formation: Add -an to back-vowel verbs and -en to front-vowel verbs, following strict vowel harmony. almak → alan (taking), vermek → veren (giving), kalmak → kalan (remaining).
- Usage: This participle describes ongoing or habitual actions and modifies nouns as an adjective. çalışan arkadaş (working friend), satılan ayakkabılar (shoes being sold), okuyan öğrenciler (students reading).
- Negative: Insert -ma or -me between the root and the participle. gelmEyen (not coming), çalışMAyan (not working).
- Questions: Use intonation or add a question particle after the participle. Gelen kız senin arkadaşın mı? (Is the girl coming your friend?).

## The -dık/-duk/-tık/-tuk Participle: Past Definite Actions

This form indicates actions that were completed definitely in the past. It is more specific than -an/-en for describing completed past events.

- Formation: Add the suffix following vowel harmony. Back-vowel verbs: almak → aldığı (that took), kalmak → kaldığı (that remained). Front-vowel verbs: vermek → verdiği (that gave), gelmek → geldiği (that came). The participle form usually appears with a possessive marker showing who performed the action.
- Usage: Describes completed past actions with emphasis on their definiteness. geçen yıl aldığım kitap (the book I bought last year), dün gelen ziyaretçiler (the visitors who came yesterday), yaptığınız iş (the work you did).
- Negative: Insert -ma or -me between the root and the suffix. gelMEdiği (that did not come), çalışMADığı (that did not work).
- Questions: Add a question particle. Geçen ayda okuduğu romanlar harika mıydı? (Were the novels he read last month wonderful?).

## The -acak/-ecak Participle: Future Actions

This form indicates actions that will happen or are intended to happen. It also expresses possibility and capacity.

- Formation: Add -acak to back-vowel verbs and -ecak to front-vowel verbs. almak → alacak (will take), vermek → verecek (will give), gelmek → gelecek (will come), okumak → okuyacak (will read).
- Usage: Describes future or intended actions and also what someone is able or capable of doing. yarın yazacağım rapor (the report I will write tomorrow), gelecek hafta (next week), yapacağı planlar (the plans he will make).
- Negative: Insert -ma or -me between root and suffix. gelmEyecek (will not come), çalışMAyacak (will not work), vermEyecek (will not give).
- Questions: Add a question particle. Yarın okuyacağınız metni zor bulur musunuz? (Do you find the text you will read tomorrow difficult?).

## Negative Forms and Questions

Negative participles are formed uniformly across all three types by inserting -ma or -me (following vowel harmony) between the verb root and the participle ending.

- Structure: verb root + ma/me + participle suffix. The choice of -ma or -me depends on the vowel in the verb root (back vowels take -ma, front vowels take -me).
- Examples across forms: gelmEyen (not coming), gelMEdiği (that did not come), gelmEyecek (will not come), çalışMAyan (not working), çalışMADığı (that did not work), çalışMAyacak (will not work).
- Questions use intonation or question particles (mı, mi, mu, mü) added after the participle. Gelmemesi garip mi? (Is it strange that he/she did not come?). Dün almadığı hediye nerede? (Where is the gift he did not buy yesterday?).

## Common Learner Mistakes

Learners often confuse these three forms or apply vowel harmony incorrectly.

- Mistake 1: Forgetting vowel harmony rules. Using gelan instead of gelen or verAN instead of verEN ignores the front-vowel ending of the root. Always check the last vowel of the verb root to determine the suffix.
- Mistake 2: Confusing -dık and -acak for time descriptions. Dün alacağı kitap (the book he will buy yesterday) contradicts the time frame. Use Dün aldığı kitap (the book he bought yesterday) instead.
- Mistake 3: Misplacing the negative marker. The negative -ma/-me must come immediately after the root. Gelmeyen is correct; gamelyen or gelmeyenne are incorrect.
- Mistake 4: Forgetting the possessive marker with -dık in context. Kitap okudu (he read the book) becomes okuduğu kitap (the book he read), not simply okudu kitap when used attributively.
