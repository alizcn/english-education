The passive voice in Turkish is formed by adding the suffix -il or -in to the verb stem. This perspective shift allows you to focus on the action itself and what happens, rather than who performs the action, and is essential for creating varied and natural-sounding Turkish sentences.

## When to Use the Passive Voice

- Use passive when the agent (the person doing the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from context.
- Use it to emphasize the result or action rather than the person responsible.
- Use it when you choose not to reveal or cannot name the person performing the action.

Examples:
- Kitap yazıldı. (The book was written.)
- Kapı açıldı. (The door was opened.)
- Pencere kırıldı. (The window was broken.)

## Forming the Positive Passive

- Add the suffix -il or -in directly to the verb stem, which is the verb root before the infinitive ending -mak or -mek.
- The vowel within this suffix follows vowel harmony rules, changing based on the last vowel of the stem.

Common examples:
- yazmak → yazılmak (to be written)
- gelmek → gelinmek (to be come to / visited)
- duymak → duyulmak (to be heard)
- kırmak → kırılmak (to be broken)
- dökmek → dökülmek (to be poured)

## Vowel Harmony in Passive Forms

- The -il/-in suffix adapts its vowel to match the vowel harmony of the stem.
- Back vowel stems (a, ı, o, u) trigger -ıl, -ın, -ul, or -un.
- Front vowel stems (e, i, ö, ü) trigger -il, -in, -ül, or -ün.

Vowel harmony examples:
- Stems with a: satmak → satılmak, çalmak → çalınmak
- Stems with e: vermek → verilmek, gelmek → gelinmek
- Stems with o: olmak → olunmak
- Stems with u: duymak → duyulmak, vurmak → vurulmak
- Stems with ı: kırmak → kırılmak, dışlamak → dışlanmak
- Stems with ö: dökmek → dökülmek
- Stems with ü: güçleştirmek → güçleştirilmek

## Negative and Question Forms

- To negate, add -me or -ma before the passive suffix: yapılmamak (not to be done), yazılmadı (was not written).
- To ask a question, add -mi, -mı, -mu, or -mü after the tense or mood ending: Yazıldı mı? (Was it written?), Duyulacak mı? (Will it be heard?).

Examples of negative passives:
- Kitap okunmadı. (The book was not read.)
- Ev satılmamış. (The house has not been sold.)

Examples of questions:
- Kapı kapatıldı mı? (Was the door closed?)
- Uyarılmadınız mı? (Weren't you warned?)

## Reflexive Forms with -il/-in

- Some Turkish verbs use -il/-in to form reflexive verbs (where the action is done to oneself) rather than true passives.
- yıkamak (to wash) → yıkanmak (to wash oneself / to bathe)
- giymek (to put on) → giyinmek (to get dressed / to dress oneself)
- These forms look like passives but carry reflexive meaning. Context and dictionary knowledge help distinguish them.
- With some verbs, the same form can have both reflexive and passive interpretations depending on context: yıkanmak can mean to wash oneself or to be washed.

## Common Learner Mistakes

- Forgetting vowel harmony and writing the wrong vowel in the suffix: Write yazılmak not yazelmak; write duyulmak not duyelmak. This is the most frequent error.
- Assuming all verbs can take the passive form: Verbs of emotion, position, or pure state do not easily form passives.
- Confusing passive with potential: The potential suffix -ebil/-abil means able to (yazabilir - can write), while passive describes being acted upon (yazılır - is written). These can combine: yazılabilir (can be written).
- Adding -il/-in to verbs that are inherently middle or reflexive in meaning: Verbs like gelmek, gitmek, and olmak can form passives (gelinmek, gidilmek, olunmak) but they do not describe transitive actions in their base form.
- Mixing passive with other verb suffixes: Remember that tense, mood, and aspect markers come after the passive suffix: yazılmak (infinitive), yazılıyor (present progressive), yazılacak (future), yazıldı (simple past).
