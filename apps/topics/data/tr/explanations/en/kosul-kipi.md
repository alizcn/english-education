The conditional mood in Turkish, expressed through the suffixes -sa or -se, conveys conditions, hypotheses, and hypothetical situations. It is one of the most frequently used moods and is essential for expressing "if...then" statements and polite requests. Understanding its formation and usage is crucial for intermediate Turkish learners.

## When to Use the Conditional Mood

- It expresses conditions about future possibilities: Gelse, memnun olurum (If he comes, I will be happy).
- It conveys hypothetical or contrary-to-fact situations: Zengin olsam, dünyayı gezerdim (If I were rich, I would travel the world).
- It is used in polite requests and suggestions: Yardım etsene? (Would you help me?).
- It appears in sentences introduced by eğer (if): Eğer hasta olsa, evde kalır (If he were sick, he would stay home).
- It can soften commands or suggestions: Oraya gitsek mi? (Should we go there?).

## Formation and Vowel Harmony

The conditional is formed by adding -sa or -se directly to the verb stem. The choice between -sa and -se follows Turkish vowel harmony rules.

- Verbs with back vowels (a, ı, o, u) in the final syllable take -sa: almak becomes alsam (if I take), kalkmak becomes kalksam (if I get up).
- Verbs with front vowels (e, i, ö, ü) take -se: gitmek becomes gitsem (if I go), yemek becomes yesem (if I eat).
- Some verbs undergo minor vowel changes before the conditional marker: for example, uyumak becomes uyusam (if I sleep).
- The conditional marker is added before personal endings, which follow standard Turkish patterns.

## Positive Conditional Forms

To form the positive conditional, take the verb stem and add -sa or -se, then add personal endings.

- For almak (to take): ben alsam (if I take), sen alsan (if you take), o alsa (if he takes), biz alsak (if we take), siz alsanız (if you take), onlar alsalar (if they take).
- For gitmek (to go), which takes -se: ben gitsem (if I go), sen gitsen (if you go), o gitse (if he goes), biz gitsek (if we go), siz gitseniz (if you go), onlar gitseler (if they go).
- When the verb is already in a continuous or past tense form, the conditional marker is still added: ben geliyorsam (if I am coming), ben gelmiş isem (if I have come).

## Negative Conditional Forms

To negate a conditional, insert the negative marker -ma or -me between the verb stem and the conditional suffix.

- For almak (to take): ben almasam (if I do not take), sen almazsan (if you do not take), o almasa (if he does not take), biz almazsak (if we do not take), siz almazsanız (if you do not take), onlar almazlarsa (if they do not take).
- For gitmek (to go): ben gitmesem (if I do not go), sen gitmesen (if you do not go), o gitmese (if he does not go), biz gitmesek (if we do not go), siz gitmeseniz (if you do not go), onlar gitmeseler (if they do not go).
- The negative marker is always placed before the conditional suffix, regardless of the verb's base tense.

## Questions with the Conditional

The conditional mood can express polite questions, suggestions, or requests.

- Question intonation alone creates a question: Bana yardım etsene? (Will you help me?).
- The question particle mi/mı/mu/mü can also be added: Yardım etsene mi? for a more polite, tentative version.
- The conditional softens the directness of requests: Kapıyı açar mısın? (Will you open the door?) versus Kapıyı aç sa? (Would you open the door?).
- Suggestions often use the conditional: Oraya gitsek mi? (Should we go there?) or Yapmasak mı? (Should we not do it?).

## Common Learner Mistakes and Confusions

- Confusing the conditional (-sa/-se) with the aorist: Yağmur yağarsa, yollar ıslanır (When it rains, the roads get wet—habitual) versus Yağmur yağsa, yollar ıslanır (If it were to rain, the roads would get wet—hypothetical).
- Neglecting vowel harmony: writing *gelse instead of gitse (if he goes).
- Dropping personal endings: writing *ben alsa instead of ben alsam (if I take).
- Confusing conditional and subjunctive moods: Ben gelmek çok istiyorum (I want to come) should not be rendered as *Ben gelseym istiyorum.
- Forgetting the -ma/-me marker in negatives: writing *ben gitsam instead of ben gitmesem (if I do not go).
- Confusing the conditional with the past conditional (-saydı/-seydı), which expresses a counterfactual: Gelseydim, hoşça kalırdım (If I had come, I would have stayed happy).

## Time Expressions and Context Markers

- eğer (if) introduces the conditional explicitly: Eğer hasta olsa, evde kalır (If he were sick, he would stay home).
- acaba (I wonder if) softens the conditional: Acaba gelse, ne derim? (I wonder, if he comes, what I will say?).
- sonra (then) or o zaman (at that time) follow the conditional clause: Eğer gelse, o zaman kına gecesi yapabiliriz (If he comes, then we can have the henna night).
- eğer is optional: Gelse, memnun olurum is equally correct for "If he comes, I will be happy."
- Time expressions like yarın (tomorrow) or bu akşam (this evening) appear with conditionals: Yarın yağmur yağsa, pikniği iptal ederiz (If it were to rain tomorrow, we would cancel the picnic).
- The conditional pairs well with conditional or future tense in the main clause: Gelse, onu karşılayacağım (If he comes, I will greet him).
